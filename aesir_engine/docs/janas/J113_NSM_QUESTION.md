### 0.1 The question, and the inventory from this tree

The question has two halves, and the road's own records already separate them:

1. **The NSM dressing** — `janas-nsm` itself: a single-paper research instrument that produces NSM (Natural Semantic Metalanguage) explications from one trained model (DeepNSM: Llama-3.2-1B + the arXiv 2505.11764 adapter) and scores them as that paper scores (primes/molecules shares, legality, circularity), reproducing the paper's figures on its 1,341 published records. The Part I Concordance graded it REFUSED (row 69); J-099 re-confirmed the refusal as a candidate and assigned this slice the filing of the final form.
2. **The transferable core** — output restriction to a permitted vocabulary: `--strict`'s mechanism, detached from the NSM semantics. The Concordance graded it WANTED (row 70): a generally useful engine feature (grammars, lexicons, controlled vocabularies), with its design at this slice and its full build at J-114.

The tree inspection at the base tip, re-verified first-hand for this slice:

- **No NSM anything exists in the tree** — the J-099 word scan over every `.mojo` found zero hits, and nothing has landed since that changes it (J-100–J-112 built bench, report, picker, saved config, manners, the OpenAI audit, streaming, the browser page, MCP, completion, services — none NSM-shaped).
- **No permitted-vocabulary restriction exists on any generation path** (J-099 T31): no vocab-subset enforcement in any sampler, session, or serve path; the facade's grammar entry raises not-implemented.
- **What does exist is the token-text constraint grain this road already proves:** `core/grammar.mojo`'s `GBNFGrammar` validates *decoded token text* against a bounded automaton (`is_token_valid` / `advance_state` / a logits mask over candidate token texts), refuses token-ID-only masking ("token IDs alone contain no grammar information"), and its `BooleanJSONGrammar` sibling holds an exact token-sequence trie inside both CUDA sessions. A vocabulary demonstrator in this same grain — decisions over decoded token text, pure and unit-tested, hosts execute — is the honest form the tree supports today.
- **No consumer waits for NSM explication anywhere** — not in the road's consumer set, not in Samtal, not in any named host. The census records no Aesir-side request for semantic-prime explications, prime/molecule scoring, or the paper's figures.

### 0.2 The NSM verdict: REFUSED (the final concordance form of row 69)

**The NSM dressing is REFUSED — not deferred.** A deferral would promise future work this road has no honest reason to expect; the refusal is the final form, filed here with its reasons and its re-open criteria, and restated at the J-118 Part VII gate and the J-146 concordance closure. The reasons, each from the road's own filed records:

1. **Its artefacts cannot be taken up.** The trained weights and data are CC BY-NC-SA and Janas itself does not ship them. Aesir could neither redistribute them nor build a shippable feature on non-commercial share-alike research artefacts it does not hold; re-training an equivalent adapter from the paper alone is not clean-room feature parity, it is a research programme of its own.
2. **It is a single-paper instrument, not an engine capability.** The tool is bound to one model + one adapter (DeepNSM on Llama-3.2-1B) and to one paper's scoring apparatus (primes/molecules shares, legality, circularity, reproduction of the paper's figures on its 1,341 published records). Reproducing a paper's figures is a research claim, not a behaviour a host can call; nothing in it generalises to Aesir's models, tokenizers, or surfaces.
3. **No Aesir consumer exists.** No named host, surface, or user need waits for NSM explications (§0.1). This road builds bindings and surfaces only where a named consumer waits (the J-115 rule, applied consistently); a research instrument with no consumer is not carried on hope.
4. **Its own published evidence does not recommend the dressing's workflow.** In Janas's published measurement, the strict (restricted) mode gains legality (+0.15) but loses neighbour-discrimination (−6.0 points), and Janas's default therefore writes freely. The restriction mechanism earns its place on its own merits (row 70, §0.3); the explication workflow built around it did not earn adoption even at its source.
5. **The semantics are the dressing.** Semantic primes, molecules, explication form, and the paper's legality/circularity judgements are a linguistic theory's apparatus. Importing the vocabulary-restriction mechanism (§0.3) takes everything transferable; importing the theory would dress an engine feature in a research programme's clothes — the exact move this road's refusals exist to prevent (compare rows 66/68: the `.jns` format and FreeBASIC refused, their cores graded separately).

**Re-open criteria (the refusal is final unless all three hold):** (a) a named Aesir consumer asks for NSM explication by name; (b) weights/data under terms Aesir can ship are actually obtainable (not merely published-about); (c) Volmarr rules the road re-opened. Until then the row stands REFUSED at the J-118 gate and at J-146, and no slice is owed.

**The core is not refused.** Row 70 stands WANTED: §0.3 builds its demonstrator here, and J-114 builds the full enforcement (exact sampler masking, measured cost, chat + server surface). Nothing in this verdict narrows J-114.

### 0.3 The demonstrator: `aesir_engine/core/vocab_restriction.mojo` (new, pure layer)

A constrained-generation **demonstrator**: the permitted-vocabulary core proven as pure decisions over decoded token text, in `core/grammar.mojo`'s grain (the layer decides; hosts — in J-114 — execute). It imports nothing from the engine; it is unit-tested at the lightest target; no session, sampler, or socket is touched or faked.

- **The word list.** `vr_parse_word_list(spec) -> PermittedVocab` (raises on a broken spec): the spec is words separated by whitespace, commas, or semicolons; entries are de-duplicated preserving first order; matching is **exact and case-sensitive** (a lexicon means what it says). Bounds, stated on the layer's face: at most `VR_MAX_ENTRIES` (8192) distinct entries, each at most `VR_MAX_WORD_BYTES` (64) bytes; an empty spec, an empty-result spec, or an over-bound entry is refused. `vr_contains(vocab, word)`, `vr_word_count(vocab)`, and `vr_render_word_list(vocab)` (the canonical comma-joined form) complete the set handling.
- **The token-text restrictor.** `VocabRestrictor` (built from a `PermittedVocab`) mirrors the grammar automaton's shape: it carries the accepted text's trailing **partial word** (the bytes since the last separator) and the completed-word count. Separators are exactly the whitespace bytes (space, tab, newline, carriage return) — **answered-locally**: punctuation is part of the word as surfaced, so a lexicon that wants `hello,` lists `hello,`; inventing punctuation rules would silently change what the list means. Its decisions:
  - `is_token_valid(token_text)`: simulate appending the decoded token text — every word the text *completes* (a separator ends it) must be a permitted word (completing an empty word — consecutive separators — is a no-op, allowed), and the trailing partial word must be a **prefix of at least one permitted word** (or empty), so an admitted token never strands generation outside the list. Empty token text is invalid, exactly as in the grammar layer.
  - `advance_state(token_text)` (raises): validate, then commit (partial word and completed count updated).
  - `is_accepting()`: the text so far could end here — the partial word is empty or itself a permitted word.
  - `allowed_indices(candidate_texts) -> List[Int]` and `first_allowed(candidate_texts) -> Int` (−1 when none): the pure selection form — which of a step's candidate token texts may be taken. This is the demonstrator's core claim, and the exact decision J-114's sampler mask must reproduce over logits.
- **The finished-text audit.** `vr_audit_text(vocab, text) -> VocabAudit` (total words, permitted words, first offending word + whether one exists): whitespace-split, exact match. This grades a *finished* text against a list (the free-written case: what share of its words the list permits) without pretending to have constrained its generation.
- **The disposition on the layer's face.** `vr_nsm_disposition()` returns the §0.2 verdict as served text (REFUSED, the five reasons in brief, the re-open criteria) — the refusal is stated where a user of the layer looks, never only in this doc (the J-112 disposition precedent).
- **A simulated generation loop is the demonstration.** The test suite drives the restrictor over synthetic candidate lists step by step (greedy first-allowed selection + `advance_state`), and files the resulting text: every emitted word in the list, a step whose candidates all violate the list stops the loop with no allowed index (−1) rather than emitting, and the audit of the demonstrated text reports zero offenders. The simulation is named a simulation wherever it is filed — it proves the *decision layer*, not a sampler.

- **Q: REFUSED or DEFERRED for the NSM dressing?** Answered in the open (§0.2/§2): REFUSED. - **Q: At what level does the demonstrator restrict — token IDs, words, or token text?** Answered locally (§0.3): decoded token text, in `core/grammar.mojo`'s grain. Admitting a partial that no permitted word extends would strand generation outside the list with no honest continuation; refusing every partial would forbid multi-token words entirely.
