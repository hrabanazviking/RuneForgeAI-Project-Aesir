**J-057 closes as an inspection + pipeline accounting slice whose accounted verdict is a REJECTION on every shape that exists tonight, NOT GRADED on the one shape that does not (the unbuilt GPU block executor), with its live leg DEFERRED-WITH-REASON** (the J-056 closure shape, with J-025/J-027's discipline of separating the accounted verdict from the unproven time case):

- **The headline is an inspection finding (§2): the stall the roadmap text imagines removing does not exist as a phase in Aesir's loops.** Every surface tokenises the whole prompt before its chunk loop, allocates its KV cache once at fixed capacity before the loop, and (on GPU) uploads weights once at open. Between two chunks, today's host loop executes a loop increment and a bounds check; today's CUDA loops execute a synchronisation and a control poll. There is no per-chunk CPU preparation phase idling a GPU — the preparation was already hoisted, or never existed per chunk, or is data-dependent on the chunk's own output (§2's inventory grades every candidate item). The one genuinely movable per-chunk item is the next chunk's token-slice staging: 2,048 B at width 256.
- **The survival verdict (§5–§6), per shape:** on the filed dense shapes the movable staging's hidden share is **0 basis points** — at parity cost (one operation per staged id against one MAC) and at a 10,000× cost multiplier per staged id, a four-order-of-magnitude sensitivity sweep, so the verdict does not rest on any invented count-to-time exchange rate. **ACCOUNTED-AND-REJECTED** (below the 10% margin) wherever a pipeline could run at all; **NO-BOUNDARY** for single-chunk prompts (≤ 256 tokens at the default cap — no internal boundary exists); **REJECTED-BY-CONSTRUCTION** on the host CPU-only loop, where preparation and compute share the same cores and "overlap" is core theft, not a pipeline; **NOT APPLICABLE as a GPU overlap** on the streamed MoE line (a CPU-only session, J-030/J-031), whose one real overlap — J-019's next-slab readahead — is already built, and whose expert-demand preparation is **not movable** (it depends on the router applied to hidden states that do not exist yet; its predictive forms are J-025's and J-027's, measured and rejected there).

## 2. Inspection — the loops as they are, and every preparation item graded (PROVEN from the tree)

**The loops.** The host engine (`aesir.mojo` `_run_generation`) encodes the whole prompt (`tokenizer.encode`) and constructs one fixed-capacity `KVCache` *before* its chunk loop, then calls `_forward_batch` strictly sequentially (`while len(tokens) - 1 - pos >= batch_cap`, cap from `_prefill_batch_cap`, default 256); the final chunk's pass yields the first sampled token. The CUDA sessions (`llama3_cuda.mojo` / `gemma4_cuda.mojo` `begin_turn`) likewise tokenise the whole turn up front, then loop per token (Llama: per tile where `prefill_four` is admitted), each iteration polling `control.stop_reason()` and ending in `context.synchronize()` (J-053 §3.8: once per token at width 1, once per tile at width 4). The streamed MoE session (`moe_session.mojo` `step`) is width-1, CPU-only, one token per call. **No loop in the tree overlaps anything with anything** — but, per the inventory below, almost nothing is waiting to be overlapped.

**The preparation inventory** — every candidate "CPU prepares what follows" item, graded by dependence:

| # | Item | Grade | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Tokenising chunk n+1's text | **HOISTED — nothing to move** | All surfaces encode the whole prompt/turn before the loop (above); chunk n+1's ids exist before chunk 0 computes |
| P2 | Token-slice staging for chunk n+1 (copying its ids / setting its slice bounds) | **MOVABLE — the only per-chunk item** | Depends only on P1's output. Countable size: `overlap_staging_bytes` — 256 ids × 8 B = **2,048 B** per boundary at width 256 (§6 grades its share) |
| P3 | KV allocation for chunk n+1 | **HOISTED — does not exist per chunk** | Host `KVCache` is fixed-capacity, constructed once before the loop; per-chunk KV *writes* are in-pass work (`append_block`, one flat copy per layer per chunk, J-053 §3.5), and their offsets are arithmetic inside the pass, not a preceding phase |
| P4 | Weight staging/upload for chunk n+1 (dense) | **DOES NOT EXIST per chunk** | Dense GPU weights upload once at open through the 64 MiB synchronised staging and stay device-resident (J-054 §2, J-055 §2); a resident chunk re-reads device memory, it is never re-staged by the CPU |
| P5 | Expert-demand preparation for chunk n+1 (MoE) | **NOT MOVABLE** | Chunk n+1's routed experts are chosen by routers applied to chunk n+1's own hidden states, which chunk n's compute does not produce. The ahead-of-time forms are prediction: the router-shadow prefetch (J-025: previous-layer overlap 0.977/8, *below* the random 1.000 expectation — measured and rejected) and cross-session map persistence (J-027: measured and rejected). The within-token form that *is* sound — J-019's reader issuing willneed advice for the next *selected* expert — is already built and shipped |
| P6 | Sampler preparation for the decode step | **Static part HOISTED; input NOT MOVABLE** | The generation config is copied and validated before prefill; the CUDA sampler records tokens as prefill commits them. What decode actually waits on — the sampled token — is the final chunk's logits output: data-dependent by construction. §4's decode-boundary account prices the shape; §6 shows its prompt-scale share |
| P7 | Launch/bounds arithmetic; cancellation/control polling | **MOVABLE in principle, O(1) per boundary** | A loop increment + comparison (host) and one `stop_reason()` poll per token (CUDA). Pure host arithmetic with no countable bulk; §6's sensitivity sweep (×10,000 per staged item) covers it generously without inventing a figure for it |
| P8 | The boundary synchronisation itself | **UNMOVABLE** | It *is* the pipeline's join point (J-054: one per chunk on the GPU shape; J-056 priced the split's analogue). An overlapped schedule still pays it; the accounting carries it in the unmovable bucket |

**Two structural corollaries.** (a) On the **host CPU-only** loop there is no second resource: preparation and compute contend for the same cores (threaded across all cores already, J-039), so an "overlapped" host schedule can only steal compute cores — rejected by construction, before any counting. (b) At **width 1** the CUDA loop's boundary *is* the per-token synchronisation (P8), and its only movable item is a single token id (P2 at width 1) — the thinnest possible pipeline, graded in §6 with the rest.

## 3. Missing pieces — what a real overlap build would need that does not exist

Bound to J-054's M-list where it gates, plus the three pieces this slice names:

- **The GPU block executor itself (J-054 M1 + M2 + M5) gates any GPU pipeline.** Overlap is a property of a chunk loop; at block width no such loop exists. At width 1 the loop exists and §6 removes the reason to pipeline it. **M7** (prefill instrumentation, J-053 F3) gates quoting any per-phase wall, hence §7 times from outside and from O2.
- **O1 — A pipeline loop with a preparation stage (NEW, named here).** A second host thread running chunk n+1's movable preparation behind a ready-handshake, with double-buffered staging (two slice buffers, so compute never reads a slice being written). Host-side only — P2/P7 need no device asynchrony. This is the machinery the §5 margin exists to gate: it is built only when a measured hidden share repays it.
- **O2 — Per-boundary prep/compute timestamps (NEW, named here).** No timer in the tree separates a chunk boundary's preparation from its compute (J-053 F3). §7's leg instruments the boundary from outside first; O2 is the in-engine form, owned with F3 by J-059's watching surface.
- **O3 — An asynchronous device-staging road (NEW, named here, conditional).** *If* a future executor ever stages device data per chunk (none does tonight — P4), hiding that staging needs an async copy road; today's upload road (`core/cuda_upload.mojo`) synchronises per 64 MiB staging chunk. Named so a later slice cannot quietly assume it; not needed by O1's host-side items.

**What is NOT missing:** the tokenised prompt (P1), the KV capacity (P3), the resident weights (P4), the one sound expert overlap (J-019's readahead, P5) — the hoisted and built pieces are why the movable remainder is so small.

## 5. The worth-building rule (pre-registered)

Stated in `core/prefill_overlap.mojo`'s header and here **before** the §6 shapes were run through it. An overlap **SURVIVES ACCOUNTING** only if all three hold: **(a)** at least two chunks exist — a single-chunk prompt has no internal boundary (**NO_BOUNDARY**); **(b)** movable preparation per boundary is non-zero (**REJECTED_NOTHING_TO_HIDE** otherwise); **(c)** the hidden fraction of the sequential total reaches the road's standing margin — `hidden_basis_points ≥ 1000` (10%, the J-040/J-043 margin discipline) — else **REJECTED_BELOW_MARGIN**: the pipeline machinery (O1's thread, handshake, double buffering) costs more complexity than a sub-margin hiding repays, exactly as J-040 keeps a default no measured winner beat by the margin.

Two honesty clauses ride with the rule. (a) **The rule grades the units it is given.** Applied to operation counts it is a *bound* argument, and §6 therefore sweeps the one exchange rate it could be accused of hiding (operations per staged id, parity → 10,000×) and shows the filed verdict invariant across it. Only a live leg's measured nanoseconds — prep and compute timed at the same boundaries, on the same runs — can promote an accounted survival into a wall claim, or reopen a rejection, and then only under the same 10% margin with round separation (§7). (b) **Per-shape verdicts stay per-shape.** A rejection on the dense resident shapes says nothing about a future executor whose preparation has never been observed (§1, §7); a survival on synthetic units in the test suite says nothing about any filed shape.

- **Why the share floors to zero so robustly:** per boundary, the movable count is O(chunk width) element operations while the compute it would hide behind is O(chunk width × per-token MACs) — a ratio of one staged id against ~7×10⁹ MACs on the 8B shape (~4.4×10⁸ on the smallest filed shape). No plausible per-operation cost closes nine (or eight) orders of magnitude; the sweep to ×10,000 still leaves the share at 0 bp with five orders to spare. That insensitivity — not any single exchange rate — is the verdict's ground.
