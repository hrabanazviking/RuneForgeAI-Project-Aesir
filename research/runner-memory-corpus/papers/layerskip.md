# LayerSkip — LayerSkip: Enabling Early Exit Inference and Self-Speculative Decoding

*Ollama Research Forge, OR-3 Paper Sweep, Wave 24 (early exit). Companion dossier: `papers/skipdecode.md`. This wave covers the decode-speed line's third family — conditional compute in **depth**: the model itself spends fewer layers on tokens that do not need full depth — after speculation (Waves 2/13: draft cheap, verify dear) and alongside sparsity. LayerSkip is the training-side member of the pair: it builds the exit capability into the weights. SkipDecode (companion) is the serving-side member: a static, batch-uniform exit budget imposed on a fine-tuned model.*

## 2. The problem as framed

- **Uniform depth wastes compute — and the waste is measurable.** The paper's motivating probe (Llama 1 7B, first HumanEval prompt) projects every layer's hidden state through the model's own LM head ("unembedding") and records when each token's final prediction first appears. On average a token's final prediction is already present by layer 23.45 of 32 — so even a *perfect, zero-cost* exit oracle could only save ~26% of compute on the untrained model. Two distinct wastes follow: easy tokens (the paper's example: a `for` starting a loop) still burn all 32 layers, and intermediate layers "change their minds" — a token predicted correctly at layer 7 can be talked out of it at layers 22–26 before settling back. Standard training gives the network no reason to decide early; it spreads its computation across all layers by default.
- **Untrained early exit breaks accuracy, catastrophically on generation.** The LM head is trained to unembed the *last* layer's output only. On the untrained baseline, exiting at the middle layer collapses open-ended generation to near zero (continual-pretraining table, §4: NaturalQuestions 25.1% → 0.06%, HumanEval 13.4% → 0%, GSM8K 14.3% → 0% on Llama 2 7B) and blows perplexity up by two to three orders of magnitude. Multiple-choice tasks degrade far less, because they score one token out of 2–4 options rather than a long chain out of a full vocabulary. So the capability to exit is not latent in an ordinary checkpoint waiting for a clever threshold — it must be *built*.
- **External speculation pays for its draft.** Speculative decoding (the corpus's Waves 2/13 family) restores exactness, but with a second, draft model: extra memory footprint, a second KV cache to maintain, and a second artefact to train and host. The paper asks whether the draft can be the *same* network's prefix — which is only possible if the prefix has been trained to predict well on its own. That is the gap the training recipe fills.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

LayerSkip is an end-to-end recipe in three parts: a training recipe, a plain early-exit inference mode, and a self-speculative decoding mode that recovers the accuracy the plain mode gives up.

**Training — layer dropout with a depth-graded schedule.** During training, each transformer layer's residual update is stochastically skipped per sample: $x_{l+1,t} = x_{l,t} + M(p_{l,t})\, f_l(x_{l,t})$, where $M$ is Bernoulli with drop probability $p_{l,t} = S(t)\,D(l)\,p_{max}$. The per-layer factor $D(l)$ grows **exponentially from 0 at layer 0 to 1 at the last layer** ($D(l) = e^{l \ln 2 / (L-1)} - 1$): early layers are almost never dropped, late layers are dropped most — the network is forced to become *less reliant on later layers*. The time factor $S(t)$ is 1 (no curriculum) for continual pretraining and fine-tuning, and an exponential ramp for pretraining from scratch. Dropped samples are removed from the batch for that layer's computation (so training itself speeds up), with the RNG seeded identically across GPUs so every device drops the same count. Reported $p_{max}$ values: 0.1 in most runs, 0.2 for from-scratch 7B and the TOPv2 fine-tune.

**Training — early-exit loss through one shared head.** At (a curriculum-selected subset of) layers, the layer's hidden state is passed through the model's *own, single* LM head $g$ and scored with the ordinary cross-entropy loss against the target: $J = \sum_l \tilde{e}(t,l)\, J_{CE}(g(x_{l+1}), Y)$. Deliberately, there are **no per-layer auxiliary heads and no added modules** — unlike Elbayad et al. and CALM, which train a dedicated head per exit. One shared head keeps training and inference memory flat and makes every prefix of the network a usable sub-model. The per-layer weights $e(l)$ grow **quadratically** with depth (each layer's scale is proportional to the sum of all previous scales, with the final layer given an extra boost), because predicting late is easier and should not dominate the gradient; the hyperparameter $e_{scale} \in [0,1]$ controls how much the early layers' losses count at all (0.1–0.2 for pretraining regimes, 1.0 for domain/task fine-tunes). Because supervising every layer every step is expensive and hurts final-layer accuracy, a curriculum $C(t,l)$ gates which layers are supervised when: either **rotational** (supervise every $R$-th layer, rotating each iteration, so each layer is supervised once every $R$ steps) or **gradual** (enable layers one at a time from the top down). The trained result is best understood as an *ensemble of nested sub-models of every depth inside one checkpoint*.

**Inference — plain early exit at a fixed layer $E$.** Generation runs the first $E$ layers and jumps straight to the LM head: output $= g(x_E)$. **Correction to a common summary of this paper (and to this wave's brief): LayerSkip's early exit is *not* confidence-thresholded.** The paper contains no per-token confidence rule for choosing $E$; $E$ is a fixed, per-deployment choice swept in the experiments, and the accuracy cost of a wrong (too-early) $E$ is exactly what self-speculation exists to repair. Confidence heuristics appear in the paper only as *prior work* (Zhang et al. 2019), and per-token dynamic exit conditions (as in CALM) are named as *future work* in the conclusion. Any dossier or summary attributing a confidence threshold to LayerSkip is describing a different paper.

**Inference — self-speculative decoding.** The draft model is the verifier's own prefix; the verifier is the same network's remainder:

1. *Self-drafting.* Run autoregressively with exit at layer $E$ to draft $d$ tokens cheaply (the "number of speculations"; $d$ ranges 4–12 in the experiments). Because training produced a usable sub-model at *every* depth, $E$ is a free latency/quality dial chosen per task.
2. *Self-verification.* Run the remaining $L - E$ layers over the drafted span in **one parallel forward pass**, exactly as in standard speculative verification: accept the longest prefix where draft and full-model predictions agree, emit the next full-model token at the first disagreement, and re-draft from there. The output distribution is the full model's — self-speculation is lossless in the same sense external speculation is (§5).
3. *Cache reuse (KVQ).* Because draft and verification execute the *same early layers in the same order* — the structural advantage over Zhang et al.'s Draft & Verify, which skips *middle* layers and therefore cannot reuse — the two stages share a **single KV cache**: the first $E$ layers' keys and values computed while drafting are exactly what verification needs. On top of that, an **exit query cache** stores the query vector of the exit layer ($E-1$) so verification can resume directly at layer $E$ without recomputing the prefix; KV cache + exit query together are the paper's "KVQ cache". The ablation (§4) credits KVQ reuse with saving 9–20 ms/token on CPU inference in their setup.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All numbers are the paper's own (ACL 2024 / arXiv v4 text, read 2026-11-15), in the paper's frame. Models: Llama-family only. Training regimes: continual pretraining (Llama 2 7B on 52B tokens; Llama 2 13B; later Llama 3 8B on 419B tokens and Llama 3.2 1B on 839B tokens), pretraining from scratch (custom Llama 2 1.5B and Llama 2 7B on 26B tokens), code fine-tuning (Llama 1 7B on 5.2B CodeLlama-mix tokens), task fine-tuning (Llama 1.5B on TOPv2). Self-speculation measured with greedy decoding, max 512 generated tokens, Hugging Face implementation; hardware **NVIDIA H100** for most generation results, **A100** for the code fine-tune, CPU for the KVQ ablation.

**Headline speedups (abstract / §6.2): self-speculative decoding, 1.34×–2.16× over autoregressive decoding with the untrained-recipe baseline, with full-model quality restored by verification.** By cell:

- *Continual pretraining, CNN/DM 1-shot summarisation:* Llama 2 7B, $E{=}8$, $d{=}12$: ROUGE-2 0.078 (autoregressive 0.079), token acceptance 68.9%, 127.9 tok/s vs 62.7 → **1.86×**. Llama 2 13B, $E{=}15$, $d{=}12$: ROUGE-2 0.098 = autoregressive, acceptance 74.5% → **1.81×** (vs Draft & Verify's 1.56× on the same cell, as reported there).
- *XSUM 0-shot:* 7B **1.54×** (acceptance 54.6%), 13B **1.34×** (acceptance 67.7%; Draft & Verify 1.48× — the one cell where the paper reports itself slower than the middle-skip baseline).
- *HumanEval:* 7B **1.83×**, 13B **1.66×**, ROUGE-2 equal to autoregressive in both cells as reported.
- *Pretraining from scratch, CNN/DM:* 1.5B **1.76×**, 7B **2.16×** — the paper's maximum, noted as exceeding traditional speculative decoding in that setting.
- *Code fine-tune, HumanEval:* exit $E{=}6$, $d{=}12$: **1.82×** "with no accuracy drop" (ROUGE-2 0.0513, identical to autoregressive as reported; acceptance only 45%).
- *TOPv2 fine-tune:* self-speculation at $E{=}6$ reaches **2.0×** with EM 82.9% vs autoregressive 85.9% — note this cell is *not* quality-identical as reported; acceptance rises steeply with $E$ (76.0% at $E{=}6$, 97.2% at $E{=}12$, 98.9% at $E{=}18$).

**Plain early exit (no verification) is fast but lossy, as reported.** In the continual-pretraining table, early exit alone runs ~3.5× the autoregressive token rate (e.g. 232.4 vs 62.7 tok/s) but ROUGE-2 collapses (CNN/DM 0.079 → 0.012; XSUM 0.073 → 0.002). On TOPv2, plain exit EM falls with $E$: 83.3% ($E{=}18$), 79.4% ($E{=}12$), 62.9% ($E{=}6$) vs 85.9% full — while the *untrained* baseline scores 0 EM at every reduced depth. The training recipe's own effect, same table family: middle-layer perplexity on Wikipedia falls from 1900 (baseline) to 8.12 (LayerSkip) for Llama 2 7B, and middle-layer task scores recover much of the way (BoolQ 62.2 → 75.7 vs 77.4 full; MMLU 38.9 → 40.2 vs 46.0 full) — recovered, not equalled. Final-layer cost of the recipe on Llama 2 is small as reported (MMLU 46.0 → 43.1 at 7B; 55.2 → 53.7 at 13B); on Llama 3 8B the paper reports a *larger* final-layer drop (MMLU 66.5 → 60.5; HumanEval 37.8 → 28.7) and itself suggests the recipe belongs in from-scratch pretraining of future models rather than as a late patch on heavily trained ones.

**Stated limitations (the paper's own §8):** self-speculation requires fine-tuning or pretraining with the recipe (the competing Draft & Verify needs no weight changes); $p_{max}$, $e_{scale}$ and $R$ need tuning to protect final-layer accuracy; from-scratch layer dropout needs a raised, carefully tuned learning rate.

## 5. Position in the family

- **Self-speculation vs external drafting (Waves 2/13 — speculative decoding, SpecInfer, EAGLE).** The verify mathematics is *the same*: draft a span cheaply, verify it in one parallel pass through the full model, accept the agreeing prefix, correct at the first disagreement; the served distribution is the full model's either way. What changes is only the **draft source**. External drafting (Leviathan/Chen speculative sampling, SpecInfer, EAGLE-2 in this corpus) uses a *separate* artefact — a smaller sibling model, or a trained draft head grafted onto the target. LayerSkip's draft is the verifier's own first $E$ layers. Consequences, in both directions: there is **no second model to host** — no extra weights in memory, no second KV cache, and draft/verify share activations and KVQ state because they are literally the same layers run in the same order; but the draft's quality is **bounded by early-layer competence** — the first $E$ layers of a network are a much weaker predictor than a small complete model of comparable cost *unless the network was trained to make them one*, which is the entire point of the recipe. EAGLE's draft head is the middle point of this spectrum: a small trained addition that predicts *features*, not tokens, and still needs the target resident. LayerSkip removes the addition and moves the cost into the checkpoint's own training.
- **Early exit vs Mixture-of-Experts (Wave 6).** Both are **conditional compute** — spend the network selectively instead of uniformly — but along orthogonal axes. MoE conditions on the token in **width**: every token traverses every layer, but at each layer a router sends it to a subset of expert FFNs. Early exit conditions on the token (or, in LayerSkip-as-published, on the deployment's chosen $E$) in **depth**: the token traverses a subset of *layers*, each in full width. The kinship is the shared premise — most tokens do not need the whole network — and the shared price: the conditional structure must be trained in (MoE routers and experts are architectural; LayerSkip's exit competence is a training recipe), and both complicate serving regularity (MoE: expert load imbalance; naive early exit: ragged batches — the problem the companion dossier exists to solve). They compose in principle: nothing in either paper forbids an MoE model whose tokens also exit early.
- **Within early exit itself:** LayerSkip is the *capability* paper — it makes prefixes predictive. It deliberately does not ship a per-token exit *policy* (no confidence rule, no learned exit classifier, no added heads). CALM is the policy paper for encoder–decoder models; SkipDecode (companion) is the policy paper for batched decoder-only serving. The three are complements, not competitors.

## 6. Applicability

**(a) The retrofit question — answered plainly. LayerSkip is trained in; there is no retrofit.** The paper's own limitation section states it without hedging: the method "requires finetuning a model or pretraining it with our recipe." The mechanism *is* the training — layer dropout with the exponential depth schedule plus the shared-head early-exit loss. An off-the-shelf checkpoint (a stock Llama GGUF, say) was trained with neither, and the paper's own baseline columns show what exiting such a model costs: generation quality at half depth is effectively zero. **Judgment (ours, labelled as such):** for existing models LayerSkip is — exactly like Native Sparse Attention in this corpus — a **detect-and-honour property of future checkpoints**, not a runner feature. If a future checkpoint is released trained with this recipe (the authors open-sourced code and checkpoints), a runner's job is to *honour* it: expose the exit layer $E$ and the speculation depth $d$ as serving parameters, implement the single-KV-cache/KVQ-resume path, and verify with the remaining layers. A runner cannot confer the property on a checkpoint that lacks it, and any runner option that simply truncates an untrained model at layer $E$ and calls it "early exit" is selling the §4 baseline column — perplexity in the thousands — as a feature.

**(b) What a runner can do today — honestly, nothing, for early exit proper.** Without the trained capability there is no honest early-exit mode to ship. The one legitimate runner-side relative must not be conflated with it: **confidence-based *stopping*** — halting *generation* when the model is confidently done (EOS handling, stop strings/sequences, entropy- or repetition-triggered termination of the whole sequence) — saves *tokens*, by ending the sequence sooner; early exit saves *layers per token*, by shortening each forward pass. They are different axes, different mechanisms, different risk profiles; a runner that already does the first has done nothing toward the second. A second honest non-feature is worth naming because it is often confused with exit: running a *smaller complete model* (a lower quant, a smaller sibling) is model substitution, not depth-conditional compute. **Judgment (ours):** the correct runner-side posture today is detection and metadata — record, per checkpoint, whether it declares LayerSkip-style training (exit-competent prefixes, shared head), the way the corpus argues runners should record NSA-style attention properties — so that if such checkpoints reach the GGUF ecosystem, the serving path (§6a) can be switched on deliberately rather than improvised.

## 7. Cross-links

- **`papers/specinfer.md` and `papers/eagle-2.md` (Waves 2/13, external speculation).** Self-speculation is the zero-extra-model point of the speculation family: same draft/verify/correct loop, same losslessness argument, draft source moved inside the verifier. SpecInfer's tree attention and EAGLE-2's dynamic draft trees both spend effort making an *external* draft propose better spans; LayerSkip spends the effort at *training time* making the internal draft worth verifying. A runner supporting external speculation already owns most of the machinery self-speculation needs (span verification, acceptance bookkeeping) — the new parts are the shared KVQ cache and the $E$/$d$ dials.
- **`papers/native-sparse-attention.md` (NSA).** The corpus's other trained-in property: NSA's sparse attention pattern is likewise baked in during training and likewise cannot be retrofitted onto an arbitrary checkpoint by a runner. Together NSA and LayerSkip define the corpus's **"architecture properties a runner must detect and honour"** class — capabilities that live in the checkpoint, where the runner's honest work is recognition, faithful implementation of the serving path, and refusal to fake the property where it is absent. NSA conditions compute in the *attention span*; LayerSkip in *depth*; SkipDecode (companion) adds the third member's nuance — a property conferred by fine-tuning under a static policy rather than by pretraining architecture.
- **`papers/dola.md` (Wave 23).** DoLa and LayerSkip are the two corpus papers that treat *early layers as an information source*, in opposite directions. DoLa **reads** them: it projects intermediate hidden states through the head and *contrasts* the premature layer against the final layer to amplify late-arriving factual signal — and it can do so on an *untrained-for-exit* model precisely because it never believes the early distribution, only differences it against the final one. LayerSkip **stops at** them: it trains until the early distribution is worth believing outright, then either serves it (plain exit, lossy) or drafts with it (self-speculation, lossless). The kinship is the shared instrument — the logit lens / unembedding probe is DoLa's mechanism and LayerSkip's motivating measurement (§2) — and the contrast is the cleanest illustration in the corpus of the retrofit line: reading early layers is a decode-time trick any runner can attempt; exiting at them is a training property.
