# DoLa — DoLa: Decoding by Contrasting Layers Improves Factuality in Large Language Models

*Ollama Research Forge, OR-3 Paper Sweep, Wave 23 (decoding for truth). Companion dossier: `papers/contrastive-decoding.md`. ## 2. The problem as framed

DoLa's problem statement is narrower and sharper than its companion's: not generation quality in general, but **hallucination** — "generating content that deviates from facts seen during pretraining" — in deployments where no retrieval and no fine-tuning are available to correct it.

- **The knowledge is in the network; the output distribution does not privilege it.** The paper's interpretability premise, drawn from the work it cites: transformer layers encode different kinds of information at different depths — lower-level (e.g. part-of-speech) information earlier, more semantic information later; "knowledge neurons" concentrated in topmost layers; factual associations editable by manipulating specific feedforward layers. Factual knowledge is *localised*, unevenly, across depth.
- **Likelihood training works against surfacing it.** The paper attributes hallucination in part to the maximum-likelihood objective itself: mass-seeking behaviour that assigns non-zero probability to sentences not fully consistent with the knowledge in the training data, and a model that learns to "recognize the superficial patterns, instead of recognizing and generating the real-world facts." At decode time the consequence is concrete: a syntactically plausible but false token (the paper's Figure 1 example: "Seattle," as the capital of Washington state) can hold roughly constant, high probability through *every* layer — it was never a knowledge problem, it is a fluency answer — while the true token ("Olympia") only gains probability in the *later* layers, as factual knowledge is progressively injected. The final distribution mixes both histories together, and the fluent falsehood can win.
- **The fix can live at decode time.** If the factual signal is specifically the part of the final distribution that *arrived late* — the change between what an earlier layer believed and what the final layer believes — then subtracting the earlier belief should cancel the always-present fluency component and amplify exactly the late-arriving factual component. No retraining, no retrieved external knowledge, no additional model: the contrast is *inside* one forward pass.

Both Wave 23 papers thus share a skeleton — likelihood decoding amplifies the model's most generic tendencies; knowledge the network demonstrably holds is not privileged by its output distribution; a decode-time contrast can privilege it — and differ in where they find the "generic tendencies" specimen: CD (companion dossier) in a smaller model, DoLa in the same model's shallower self.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

**Early-exit logits.** A transformer with $N$ layers normally applies the vocabulary head $\phi(\cdot)$ only to the final hidden state: $p(x_t \mid x_{<t}) = \mathrm{softmax}(\phi(h_t^{(N)}))$. DoLa also applies the *same* head to earlier layers' hidden states — the "early exit" construction — giving, for each candidate layer $j$, a full next-token distribution $q_j(x_t \mid x_{<t}) = \mathrm{softmax}(\phi(h_t^{(j)}))$. Residual connections make hidden states evolve gradually, so these intermediate distributions are meaningful without any special training. The final layer $N$ is named the **mature layer**; the chosen earlier layer $M$ is the **premature layer**.

**Premature-layer selection — dynamic, per token, by divergence maximisation.** $M$ is not fixed. At *each decoding step*, DoLa selects:

$$M = \arg\max_{j \in \mathcal{J}} \; \mathrm{JSD}\bigl(q_N(\cdot \mid x_{<t}) \,\|\, q_j(\cdot \mid x_{<t})\bigr)$$

where JSD is the Jensen–Shannon divergence and $\mathcal{J}$ is a candidate set of layers. The rationale: the layer whose distribution differs *most* from the final layer's is the layer just before the largest change — contrasting across that change maximally amplifies whatever the intervening layers added, which, when the pending token needs factual knowledge, is the factual knowledge. The paper's motivating analysis (LLaMA-7B) shows the two regimes this exploits: for fact-demanding tokens (named entities, dates) JSD against the final layer stays extremely high even into the upper layers — the model is *still changing its mind* late — while for function words and copied tokens JSD collapses to near zero by the middle layers, the decision already made.

**Buckets.** Searching all layers per token is neither necessary nor, for hyperparameter purposes, desirable. Layers are partitioned into **2–4 buckets** by depth, one bucket is chosen as $\mathcal{J}$ per task by validation, and within the bucket only **even-indexed layers** are candidates. As specified in the paper (§3.1): LLaMA-7B (32 layers): $[0,16)$, $[16,32)$; LLaMA-13B (40): $[0,20)$, $[20,40)$; LLaMA-33B (60): $[0,20)$, $[20,40)$, $[40,60)$; LLaMA-65B (80): four buckets of 20. Bucket choice is by two-fold validation (TruthfulQA-MC, FACTOR) or a validation split (GSM8K, StrategyQA); Vicuna QA, lacking one, inherits GSM8K's bucket. The validated choices carry information in themselves (§4): *higher* buckets win for short, fact-critical TruthfulQA; *lower* buckets win for FACTOR, StrategyQA and GSM8K. A static single-layer variant (DoLa-static) is analysed and found brittle — its optimal layer flips between random validation subsets of the same dataset — while dynamic selection needs only 2–4 bucket tests instead of 16–40 layer tests and stays near the best static layer on both subsets. A random-selection control performs *worse than the baseline*, so the JSD rule itself, not the mere act of contrasting some layer, is load-bearing.

**The contrast, and the plausibility head filtering.** Given $M$, the next-token distribution is:

$$\hat{p}(x_t \mid x_{<t}) = \mathrm{softmax}\bigl(\mathcal{F}(q_N(x_t), q_M(x_t))\bigr), \qquad \mathcal{F} = \begin{cases} \log \dfrac{q_N(x_t)}{q_M(x_t)}, & x_t \in \mathcal{V}_{\text{head}} \\ -\infty, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

— formally *identical* to CD's construction (companion dossier §3), with the premature layer's distribution in the amateur's place. $\mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}$ is likewise CD's adaptive plausibility constraint, imported explicitly ("Following the Contrastive Decoding approach from Li et al. 2022"; "referred [to] as an adaptive plausibility constraint (APC) proposed in Li et al. 2022"): tokens with $q_N(x_t) \ge \alpha \max_w q_N(w)$, **$\alpha = 0.1$**, survive; the rest are excluded before the contrast can promote an implausible token out of the unstable near-zero range (false positive) or bury a confident easy token whose probability barely changes across layers (false negative). For the multiple-choice likelihood scoring, the paper replaces $-\infty$ with $-1000$ so as not to destroy the scores being compared.

**Repetition behaviour, as described.** The paper reports one systematic side effect: the DoLa distribution "sometimes [has] a higher tendency to repeat previously generated sentences, especially during generation of long sequences of chain-of-thought reasoning" — downplaying lower-layer linguistic knowledge can cost surface fluency discipline over long outputs. The shipped remedy is a plain repetition penalty (Keskar et al.) with **$\theta = 1.2$** applied during decoding; the paper reports no grammatical-incorrectness problem beyond this, and analyses the penalty in its Appendix K. Note for §5: the repetition penalty is therefore not an optional garnish in DoLa-as-published — it is part of the evaluated configuration for generation tasks (it is explicitly *not* used for multiple-choice likelihood scoring, where there is no generation to repeat).

**Cost, as reported.** Latency rises by a factor of **1.01–1.08×** across LLaMA 7B–65B (e.g. 7B: 45.4 → 48.0 ms/token; 65B: 321.6 → 324.9 ms/token) — the extra work per step is projecting candidate layers through the head and computing JSDs, with no second model and no second KV cache. Hold this number loosely: it was measured in a Hugging Face-class stack on datacentre hardware; §6a explains why the personal-runner question is not cost at all.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All numbers are the paper's own (ICLR 2024 / arXiv v2 text, read 2026-11-15), in the paper's frame. Models: **LLaMA-1 family, 7B / 13B / 33B / 65B**, base (not instruction-tuned) models. Baselines: original decoding (greedy or sampling per task); CD with LLaMA-7B as the amateur for the 13B/33B/65B experts; ITI (Inference-Time Intervention — a *trained* probe method, included on 7B). $\alpha = 0.1$, $\theta = 1.2$ throughout, as §3.

**Headline claim (abstract):** DoLa "consistently improves the truthfulness across multiple choices tasks and open-ended generation tasks," improving LLaMA-family performance on **TruthfulQA by 12–17 absolute points**.

**Table 1, selected cells (baseline → +DoLa):**

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| LLaMA-7B | 25.6 → **32.2** | 40.6 → **63.8** | 19.2 → **32.1** | 58.3 → **62.0** | 58.6 → **62.2** | 30.4/96.3/26.9/2.9 → **42.1/98.3/40.8/0.6** | 60.1 → **64.1** | 10.8 → 10.5 |

Read in the paper's own terms:

- **The 12–17 point headline is the open-ended %Truth∗Info gain** (26.9→40.8, 32.4→44.6, 31.7→49.1, 34.8→49.2 — i.e. +13.9, +12.2, +17.4, +14.4). It is achieved the right way round: truthfulness rises while informativeness stays above 90% in DoLa's rows and the refusal rate (%Reject, answering "I have no comment") stays under 10% — the paper stresses this because its CD comparison achieves truthfulness partly *by refusing* (CD's %Reject reaches 20.3 / 62.7 / 44.6 at 13B/33B/65B, collapsing its %Info and, at 33B, still losing on %T∗I despite a 81.5 %Truth figure). DoLa "reaches the performance level of ITI, which relies on supervised training with labels," with no training at all.
- **Multiple-choice truthfulness (MC2/MC3) is the largest and most consistent gain** — roughly +13 to +23 points on MC2 across sizes. MC1, the winner-takes-all variant, is the exception the paper itself flags: DoLa *loses* MC1 at 33B (31.7 → 30.5) and barely moves it at 13B/65B; the paper calls MC1 "more sensitive to fluctuations" and rests its claim on MC2/MC3, which average over all true/false answers.
- **FACTOR (long-paragraph factuality): +2–4 points**, "more effective than CD, except for 13B on Wiki" in the paper's summary of its own table (13B Wiki: CD 64.4 vs. DoLa 66.2 — the table's DoLa figure is in fact higher; the corpus records the paper's sentence and its table side by side without resolving the discrepancy further than noting both).
- **Reasoning (CoT): modest, positive, and differentiated from CD.** StrategyQA **+1–4 points** at every size, where CD mostly *worsens* it — the paper's explanation: CD's 7B amateur itself has reasoning ability, so subtracting it subtracts reasoning; DoLa's premature layers lack reasoning ability, so subtracting them does not. GSM8K **≈ +2 points** at 13B/33B/65B and a small *loss* at 7B (10.8 → 10.5).
- **Vicuna QA (GPT-4-judged, open-ended chat):** DoLa "notably outperforms the baseline, especially in the 13B and 33B models" in pairwise win/tie/loss — reported graphically (paper Figure 4), not as a single scalar.
- **Qualitative honesty:** the paper prints a counterexample against itself (its Table 3, Q3) where the baseline's refusal scores truth 1.0 / info 0.0 and DoLa's detailed-but-wrong answer scores 0.0 / 1.0. DoLa surfaces *an* answer-shaped belief from the late layers; when the late layers' belief is wrong, DoLa is wrong more confidently, not less.

**Dating caveat (shared with the companion dossier).** These are 2023 results on LLaMA-1 base models, 7B–65B, unquantised, in a research stack. No number here was measured on an instruction-tuned model, a quantised model, or a model a current personal runner serves. The mechanism (§3) is an architecture-level claim; the effect sizes are as-reported history, to be re-measured before any reliance (§6b).

## 5. Position in the decoding stack

DoLa is a **logit transform** — the same stack position as CD (companion dossier §5), with the same consequences, plus one of its own.

**Against Wave 18 (validity) and Wave 9 (truncation / temperature).** The natural composition order, by what each stage mathematically needs, is: *(i) contrast* — DoLa's log-ratio must be computed from the mature and premature layers' **raw** distributions, before any mask or truncation has renormalised either of them; the JSD layer selection likewise compares raw early-exit distributions. *(ii) Validity* — the grammar mask (`papers/xgrammar.md`) as an absolute veto ($-\infty$) over the contrasted scores: a token the grammar forbids must stay forbidden however strongly the contrast promotes it. *(iii) Truncation / temperature* — min-p / top-p / temperature over what survives, then the draw. Two qualifications, both recorded as **open, not settled, in the literature**:

1. DoLa-as-published does not compose this way — it *replaces* the chain: contrast + $\mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}$ + repetition penalty ($\theta = 1.2$) + greedy/sampling per task is the whole evaluated stack. Neither Wave 23 paper evaluates its contrast stacked under a grammar mask or over a Wave 9 sampler chain, and $\mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}$ (an $\alpha$-ratio-to-max gate, $\alpha = 0.1$) overlaps in shape and purpose with min-p's floor — whether running both is redundant, harmless, or interfering is unpublished.
2. The repetition penalty's position is unspecified relative to a mask: a penalty applied to contrasted scores can in principle push a *required-by-grammar* repeated token below an invalid alternative if the mask is applied before the penalty rather than after. The safe order (mask last among score transforms, as the veto) is an engineering judgement, flagged here as such.

**The speculative-decoding break (shared with CD, restated for DoLa).** Speculative verification's acceptance rule guarantees output distributed *exactly as the target model's final-layer distribution*. DoLa's sampled distribution is by construction *not* that distribution — it is a function of two layers' distributions and a per-token argmax-over-JSD selection between them. Verifying a draft against $\hat{p}$ would require the verifier to reproduce the premature-layer selection and both early-exit projections for every position, and a correction term re-derived for the contrasted target. **Constrained / contrasted speculation is a research gap**: no published acceptance rule known to this corpus preserves the DoLa (or CD) distribution exactly. The dossier notes the gap; it does not solve it. A build combining DoLa with speculation is choosing speed over the exactness that is speculation's reason to exist, and should say so in its own documentation if it ever does.

**DoLa-specific stack note.** DoLa also breaks a quieter assumption the rest of the stack makes: that *one* distribution per step exists to be transformed. DoLa's pipeline carries $|\mathcal{J}| + 1$ distributions per step (every candidate layer's early-exit distribution, for the JSD computation) and collapses them to one only at the contrast. Any sampler-pipeline abstraction (see §7, IDEA-LEDGER #37) that models a step as a single vector being filtered cannot express DoLa without growing a multi-distribution stage.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) The personal runner — a backend-capability question

*Statements in this subsection about backend behaviour are implementation facts as banked in this corpus; recommendations are labelled proposals, not adopted techniques.*

State it plainly: **DoLa cannot be implemented on the standard path of a ggml-class backend, because that path exposes only the final layer's logits.** DoLa needs, per token: the hidden states (or, equivalently, the head-projected logits) of *every candidate layer* in the chosen bucket, every step, so it can compute each $q_j$, take JSDs against $q_N$, select $M$, and form the contrast. A backend whose inference API returns one logits vector per token — which is the standard llama.cpp / ggml serving path this corpus documents — has already discarded the information DoLa is made of. No sampler setting, plugin, or post-hoc trick on the final logits can recover it: the premature distributions are not a function of the mature distribution.

DoLa is therefore gated on a **backend capability**, in a way CD is not:

- **CD is a residency question** — two ordinary models, both exposing ordinary final logits; the only question is whether the memory pool can hold the amateur beside the expert (companion dossier §6a).
- **DoLa is a capability question** — one model, no extra residency (the early-exit projections are computed from hidden states already produced by the single forward pass; the paper's 1.01–1.08× latency is the honest shape of its cost) — but the backend must be *modified or chosen* to surface per-layer logits: a fork-level change to the compute graph's outputs, with the bandwidth cost of moving $|\mathcal{J}|$ extra vocabulary-sized vectors per token out of the forward pass, on hardware where decode is bandwidth-bound. That cost is unmeasured in the paper's setting and must not be assumed negligible on a 6 GB personal card.

**This distinction is the wave's practical yield:** the two papers look like siblings — same formula, same constraint, same $\alpha$ — and present *opposite* implementation problems. CD can be prototyped in the personal runner today, at a memory price. DoLa cannot be prototyped at all until a backend exposes the layer tap, at a capability price. Any roadmap that schedules them as interchangeable "contrastive decoding" work items is wrong in a way that will only be discovered mid-build.

### (b) The accuracy commission — a testable proposal, not an adopted technique

**Label: proposal for testing. DoLa is not adopted by the build campaign on the strength of this dossier.**

DoLa is a candidate entry for the build campaign's fact-retention battery, to be *tested* under the corpus's raw-oracle discipline: teacher-forcing rank and logit gap on the raw prompt, CPU engine against CPU comparator, no template-rendered prompts, near-tie band (rank ≤ 2 with sub-logit gap) graded faithful. It is, in one respect, an unusually *testable* proposal: because the contrast is internal to one model, the battery can grade it without a second model's confound — for each test prompt, record $q_N$ and the $q_j$ ladder, compute the DoLa distribution offline from the recordings, and check (i) whether the true continuation's rank under $\hat{p}$ improves over its rank under $q_N$ specifically on fact-demanding tokens, (ii) whether it is *unchanged* on the paper's Pattern-2 tokens (function words, copies), where JSD ≈ 0 should make DoLa a no-op — a built-in control — and (iii) whether the bucket validated for short factual answers (higher layers) and the bucket validated for reasoning (lower layers) reproduce their opposite preferences on the battery's own task split, as the paper's §3.2 pattern predicts. A DoLa implementation that cannot reproduce (ii) and (iii) from recorded logits has a bug, whatever its headline score says. All of this, however, presupposes the §6a capability: the battery itself needs a per-layer-logits tap in the *grading* harness before DoLa can even be measured, which is the correct first work item if the campaign takes the proposal up.

## 7. Cross-links

- **Contrastive Decoding dossier** (`papers/contrastive-decoding.md`, this wave) — the parent method: DoLa imports CD's objective form and its adaptive plausibility constraint wholesale, and replaces CD's external amateur model with the model's own premature layer. The two dossiers' §5/§6 sections are written as a pair: same stack position, opposite implementation price.
- **Min-p dossier** (`papers/min-p-sampling.md`, Wave 9) — chain neighbour: min-p's confidence-relative floor and DoLa's imported $\mathcal{V}_{\text{head}}$ are the same ratio-to-max construction ($\alpha = 0.1$ in DoLa's configuration). §5 records the open composition question; the Min-p paper's own warning against stacking independently tuned truncators applies with full force here.
- **Mirostat dossier** (`papers/mirostat.md`, Wave 9) — the sampler surface's other chain-breaking member: a stateful controller that substitutes for the chain. DoLa breaks the chain abstraction differently — not with state across steps, but with *width* within a step (many distributions in, one out). Between mirostat, CD and DoLa, the pipeline has now met three distinct ways a decode method can fail to be a link in a linear chain.
- **XGrammar dossier** (`papers/xgrammar.md`, Wave 18) — the ordering joint: validity must veto contrast (§5), and the mask's own correctness requirement (a wrong mask is a different sampler) extends to the composed stack — a mask computed against pre-contrast distributions and applied post-contrast without care is exactly such a wrong mask.
- **Agent memory architectures** (`memory/agent-memory-architectures.md`) — the complementary honesty layer. Memory-side honesty governs what the model is *given* — context present, attributed, intact — so it is not forced to confabulate from an absence. DoLa is decode-side honesty in its purest form in this corpus: it uses *nothing* outside the single forward pass, and its entire claim is that the model already "knows" more than its output distribution admits. The complementarity is sharp here: DoLa can only surface facts that are in the weights *and* differentially present in the late layers; it does nothing for a fact that lives only in a document the agent failed to retrieve, and memory cannot fix a case where the retrieved fact is in context, in weights, and still outvoted by a fluent falsehood — which is precisely DoLa's Figure 1 case. The accuracy commission's framing ("less hallucination by the way we use memory") names one layer; this wave names the other; the battery in §6b exists so the two are never credited for each other's gains.
- **IDEA-LEDGER #37** (`synthesis/IDEA-LEDGER.md`, rank 15) — "Sampler pipeline as a first-class, ordered, per-request surface," v3 compose-or-substitute law. **DoLa is another non-chain-shaped transform, in a new shape:** mirostat substitutes for the chain (stateful, across steps); CD feeds the chain from two models (a second logits input); DoLa feeds it from *two depths of one model* (a second logits input that only a modified backend can supply). The pattern across all three: the pipeline abstraction keeps meeting transforms that do not fit a linear chain of single-distribution filters. #37's surface, if it grows to admit this wave, needs a declared multi-source stage — with its backend requirement stated in the preset itself, so a DoLa preset fails loudly on a final-logits-only backend instead of silently degrading to ordinary decoding.
