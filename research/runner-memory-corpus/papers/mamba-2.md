# Mamba-2 — Transformers are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality

## 1. Full citation

- **Title:** Transformers are SSMs: Generalized Models and Efficient Algorithms Through Structured State Space Duality — the architecture introduced by the paper is **Mamba-2**; its core layer and framework are **Structured State Space Duality (SSD)**
- **Authors:** Tri Dao (Princeton University, Department of Computer Science) and Albert Gu (Carnegie Mellon University, Machine Learning Department) — equal contribution, alphabetical by last name in the paper
- **Venue / year:** Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2024), PMLR 235:10041–10071 — https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/dao24a.html
- **arXiv:** arXiv:2405.21060 [cs.LG] — submitted 31 May 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.21060
- **Predecessor:** Gu & Dao, *Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces*, COLM 2024, arXiv:2312.00752 — companion dossier `papers/mamba.md`, whose §§2–3 are assumed here and not repeated in full

## 2. The problem as framed

Mamba (`papers/mamba.md`) had shown, six months earlier, that a selective SSM could match Transformers at small-to-medium scale with linear sequence scaling and constant inference state. The Mamba-2 paper frames what remained wrong — not with Mamba's quality, but with its *position* in the field and its *speed in practice*:

1. **Two literatures, no shared language.** Transformers and SSMs had developed as disjoint communities (paper §1): different theory, different systems optimisations, different architecture vocabularies (heads, tensor parallelism, variable-length packing on one side; scans, convolutions, HiPPO initialisation on the other). An SSM result could not easily import a Transformer optimisation, and vice versa. The paper's stated main goal is to build "a rich body of theoretical connections between structured SSMs and variants of attention" so that algorithmic and systems work transfers.
2. **Mamba-1's scan does not use the hardware's fastest units.** The selective scan is a sequence of scalar-ish recurrences. Modern GPUs do their real work in **matrix-multiplication (tensor-core) units** — the paper's §9.3 states the consequence directly: SSD, "thanks to its reformulation to use matrix multiplication as a subroutine, can exploit specialized matrix multiplication units on GPUs," while Mamba's fused associative scan "does not leverage matmul units." Attention, for all its quadratic cost, is matmuls all the way down and therefore runs on the best silicon. A linear algorithm that cannot use tensor cores can lose, in wall-clock, to a quadratic algorithm that can — at the sequence lengths people actually train at.
3. **Mamba-1's state was small, and recall is state-limited.** Mamba-1 used state dimension $N = 16$ per channel, because its scan's cost grows linearly in $N$. The recall evidence accumulating around it (MQAR, phonebook — see `papers/mamba.md` §5.2) said recall capacity tracks state size. An algorithm whose cost is insensitive to $N$ would let the architecture buy recall with state instead of with attention layers — if such an algorithm existed.
4. **The underlying trade is unchanged.** The paper restates it in its first paragraph (§1): attention scales "quadratically in sequence length during training" and requires "a cache of size linear in sequence length during autoregressive generation," while SSMs offer "linear scaling in sequence length during training and constant state size during generation." Mamba-2 does not renegotiate that trade; it tries to get a better price on the SSM side of it — and, in §9.2.3, to price the hybrid middle precisely.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

### 3.1 The duality: one matrix, two computations

Start from the observation that any causal sequence transformation can be written as multiplying the input sequence by a lower-triangular matrix: $y = Mx$, where $M_{ji}$ says how much token $i$ contributes to output $j$. Attention builds such a matrix explicitly (scores, masked to be causal) — hence quadratic cost. A linear recurrence builds no matrix at all; it *applies* one implicitly, one step at a time — hence linear cost.

The paper's bridge is a classical class of matrices: **structured semiseparable matrices**. A (lower-triangular) semiseparable matrix is one whose off-diagonal blocks are all low-rank — each block below the diagonal factors as (something depending on the row range) × (something depending on the column range), with the factor width equal to a rank $N$. The paper proves (its §3) the correspondence that gives the framework its name:

> **SSMs with state dimension $N$ are exactly multiplications by $N$-semiseparable matrices, and vice versa.** The recurrence is a factorised way to apply the matrix; the matrix is the unrolled form of the recurrence.

Through that bridge, a second correspondence falls out (§§4–5): certain SSMs are dual to a form of attention the paper calls **structured masked attention (SMA)** — attention whose scores are multiplied by a structured mask with semiseparable structure. The intersection of the two families — models that possess *both* a linear recurrent form *and* a quadratic attention-like form — is **State Space Duality (SSD)**. Two clarifications the paper itself is careful about, and which any citation of its title should preserve:

- The duality is with **variants of attention — in the SSD case, masked *linear* attention** (scores of the form $C B^\top$ with a structured decay mask), **not softmax attention**. Softmax has no finite-dimensional factorisation of this kind; "Transformers are SSMs" is, in the paper's own footnote, an homage to Katharopoulos et al.'s *Transformers are RNNs* (linear attention), and its precise content is the semiseparable correspondence, not an identity between Mamba and GPT-style softmax attention.
- Duality is an *algorithm-design* tool: the same layer can be computed in whichever form is cheaper for the shape at hand — recurrent (linear) for decode and for long stretches, quadratic/matmul (attention-like) for parallel training — and, crucially, in a **chunked mixture** of the two (§3.3).

### 3.2 The restriction that buys everything: scalar-identity $A$

SSD does not hold for arbitrary SSMs. It holds for the subclass where the state transition is a **scalar times the identity**:

$$A_t = a_t I$$

i.e. at each step, *every* coordinate of the state decays by the *same* factor $a_t$ (equivalently, in the continuous parameterisation, $A$ is diagonal with all entries equal per head, modulated by the input-dependent $\Delta_t$ inherited from Mamba-1's selectivity). $B_t$ and $C_t$ remain input-dependent, as in Mamba-1, so the layer is still a *selective* (time-varying) SSM — the paper works throughout with the time-varying form $h_t = A_t h_{t-1} + B_t x_t$, $y_t = C_t^\top h_t$.

Why this restriction is the key move: if $A_t = a_t I$, then the semiseparable factorisation collapses beautifully. The mask in the dual attention form becomes a product of cumulative decays — $M_{ji} \propto \prod_{k=i+1}^{j} a_k$ for $j \ge i$ — which is a **1-semiseparable scalar mask** that can be applied to the $C B^\top$ score matrix cheaply. The state transition no longer mixes state coordinates; all mixing happens through $B_t$ (write) and $C_t$ (read). What is given up is real: a general diagonal $A$ lets different state coordinates remember at different timescales *independently of the input gating*; scalar-identity $A$ forces one decay rate per head per step (recovered in practice by having many heads, each with its own $a_t$ trajectory — the paper's analogue of multi-head attention, §3.4). The paper's empirical position, validated in §4, is that at equal or larger $N$ the trade is decisively worth it.

### 3.3 The SSD algorithm: chunked — matmul inside, recurrence between

The algorithm that falls out of the duality (§6) is a **block decomposition** of the semiseparable matrix, and it is the part of the paper a runner engineer should internalise, because it is how every fast Mamba-2 implementation since has been structured:

1. **Split the sequence into chunks** of length $Q$ (128 or 256 in typical implementations).
2. **Inside a chunk, use the quadratic (attention-like) form:** form the intra-chunk score matrix ($C B^\top$, masked by the cumulative-decay mask) and multiply by the chunk's inputs — dense matmuls, tensor-core work.
3. **Between chunks, use the recurrent form:** carry a single state $h$ from chunk to chunk; each chunk contributes its end-of-chunk state update (also expressible as matmuls against the chunk's $B$ and decay factors) and receives the incoming state's contribution to its outputs (another matmul, $C$ against $h$).
4. Total work is linear in $L$ at fixed $Q$ and $N$, but the constant is now dominated by matmul throughput rather than scan throughput.

Two consequences the paper measures (§4): the algorithm is **2–8$\times$ faster than Mamba-1's optimised fused scan**, and its cost grows only weakly with state dimension — it can afford **$N = 64$–$256$ where Mamba-1 used $N = 16$** (the paper: SSD allows "much larger recurrent state sizes ($8\times$ the size of Mamba or even higher, with minimal slowdown)"; Figure 10 right shows the Mamba scan slowing linearly as $N$ grows while SSD stays nearly flat). Larger $N$ at no speed cost is the paper's direct answer to the MQAR/state-capacity limitation.

### 3.4 The Mamba-2 block: attention's vocabulary, imported

Using the duality as a design language (§7), the paper reworks the Mamba block:

- **Heads.** The SSM is reorganised into explicit heads, directly analogous to multi-head attention: head dimension $P$ is widened to **64 or 128** (Mamba-1 effectively ran $P = 1$ per channel with many channels), and $B, C$ can be **shared across groups of heads** — the paper names the resulting variants by analogy with MQA/GQA (multi-value attention / grouped-value attention; cf. `papers/gqa.md`). State size per head is $N$; total state per layer is (heads $\times P \times N$).
- **Parallel projections.** All input-dependent projections ($X, B, C, \Delta$) are produced **in parallel at the start of the block**, rather than sequentially as in Mamba-1 — which halves the tensor-parallel synchronisation points per block and makes the block Megatron-style tensor-parallel friendly.
- **Extra normalisation** before the output projection (RMSNorm in the gated branch), which the ablations (Table 4) credit, together with the parallel projections, with a perplexity improvement at ~130M scale (11.76 for the Mamba-1-style sequential block without extra norm → **11.49** for the Mamba-2 parallel block with it).
- As in Mamba-1, the block retains a short causal convolution and gating; the architecture remains a homogeneous stack with no attention and no separate MLP in its pure form — the hybrid variants are experiments (§4), not the definition.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with hardware / model context

All figures are the paper's own. Efficiency hardware is an **A100 80 GB PCIe**, as in the Mamba-1 paper. Language experiments use **the Pile, GPT-NeoX tokeniser, 300B tokens** for the downstream tables, and a Chinchilla-protocol scaling study at sequence length **8,192**.

- **Headline speed claim (abstract).** Mamba-2's core layer is "a refinement of Mamba's selective SSM that is **2–8$\times$ faster**, while continuing to be competitive with Transformers on language modeling."
- **SSD vs scan, precisely (§9.3, Figure 10).** The dedicated SSD implementation is **2–8$\times$ faster than Mamba-1's optimised fused selective scan at state expansion $N = 64$**, and — the comparison that locates it against attention — **faster than FlashAttention-2 from sequence length 2K upward, and $6\times$ faster at sequence length 16K** (introduction's summary of the same benchmarks). The paper adds an honest whole-model caveat in §9.3: a full Mamba-2 model need not train faster than a Transformer at *short* lengths (~2K), because at equal parameter count it has more sequence-mixing layers and fewer of the extremely hardware-efficient MLP layers — one reason the MLP-interleaved hybrid (§ below) also trains well.
- **Scaling laws (Figure 9).** Models ~125M to ~1.3B, Chinchilla protocol on the Pile: **Mamba-2 matches or exceeds both Mamba-1 and Transformer++ in perplexity, and Pareto-dominates Transformer++ on perplexity vs both theoretical FLOPs and wall-clock time.**
- **Downstream, zero-shot (Table 1).** At each size Mamba-2 beats Mamba-1 and "generally matches Pythia at twice the model size." The largest row, quoted in full because it is the paper's flagship comparison: **Mamba-2-2.7B — Pile perplexity 6.09, LAMBADA perplexity 4.10, average accuracy 60.2** across LAMBADA/HellaSwag/PIQA/ARC-E/ARC-C/WinoGrande/OpenBookQA — vs **Mamba-2.8B at 6.22 / 4.23 / 59.9** and **Pythia-6.9B at ~59.4 average** (Table 10 full results). The paper's §1 summary: "Mamba-2 with 2.7B parameters trained on 300B tokens on the Pile outperforms Mamba-2.8B, Pythia-2.8B and even Pythia-6.9B."
- **Associative recall (§9.1, Figure 8).** On a deliberately harder MQAR setup (longer sequences, smaller models, following Arora et al.): **Mamba-1 struggles; Mamba-2 performs well across settings — better even at controlled state size ($N = 16$), for reasons the authors state they do not fully understand — and increasing $N$ from 16 to 64 to 256 consistently improves MQAR**, "as the larger state allows more information (key-value pairs) to be memorised." Read alongside `papers/mamba.md` §5.2: this is mitigation by capacity, exactly as the theory predicts, not a refutation of the capacity bound.
- **Hybrid experiments (§9.2.3, Tables 2–3) — the paper's own bridge to production.** (i) At 350M / 48 layers / 7B tokens, adding attention layers to a pure Mamba-2 stack improves Pile perplexity monotonically to a best around **~10% attention layers** (0 attention: 8.60; 6 of 48: **8.26**; 24 of 48: 8.34; pure Transformer++: 8.68). The authors' interpretation: "attention layers act as a retrieval mechanism to quickly refer to previous tokens in the sequence instead of forcing the model to compress all the context to its memory (SSM states)." (ii) At 2.7B / 300B tokens: pure Mamba-2 averages **60.2**, Transformer++ **60.2**, **Mamba-2-Attention (58 SSD + 6 attention layers) averages 61.0 with the best Pile perplexity (5.95)**, and Mamba-2-MLP-Attention 60.7. Placement of the attention layers, if spaced out and not at the very start or end, mattered little in their small-scale tests.

## 5. What it changed in practice

- **Mamba-2 became the default recurrent layer of the hybrid era.** The hybrids catalogued in `papers/mamba.md` §5.1 divide by generation: Jamba and Zamba-1 were built on Mamba-1 (both March–May 2024, before or simultaneous with this paper); essentially everything after is Mamba-2-lineage — Zamba2, NVIDIA's 8B study and the Nemotron-H family built on it, Falcon-H1, and the Mamba-2 block as the reference "SSM layer" in serving frameworks. The reasons are the ones in §§3–4: tensor-core speed made training cost competitive at ordinary lengths, and $N$ = 64–256 state made the recall gap small enough for a thin attention minority to close.
- **The delta-rule descendants — Gated DeltaNet — are this paper's grandchildren, and they are what the corpus actually serves.** Yang, Wang & Kim / Songlin Yang et al., *Gated Delta Networks: Improving Mamba2 with Delta Rule* (ICLR 2025), keep Mamba-2's gating (the scalar decay $a_t$) and replace its additive state write with a **delta-rule (error-correcting) update**: writing a new value for a key first erases the state's current association for that key, so associations are *replaced* rather than *accumulated and diluted*. Qwen's team state that in their experiments Gated DeltaNet showed stronger in-context learning than Mamba-2 or sliding-window attention, and built **Qwen3-Next** on it at a **3:1 ratio (75% Gated DeltaNet, 25% gated attention)** — 80B total / ~3B active, 48 layers, native 262,144 context (Qwen, September 2025). The corpus's **qwen35** models are of this same family: Gated-DeltaNet recurrent layers with full attention at intervals (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6.2). For a runner, the operational point is that "Mamba-2 support" in 2025–2026 practice usually means *this* lineage — gated, delta-rule, chunked — not the literal 2024 block.
- **SSD's chunked form became the implementation contract.** Because intra-chunk computation is matmul and inter-chunk computation is a state hand-off, Mamba-2 kernels, and the serving systems built on them, all expose the same seams: a **prefill/chunked-prefill path** (process a prompt chunk by chunk, carrying state) and a **decode path** (one-token recurrent step against resident state). SGLang's hybrid serving — a per-token KV pool for attention layers beside a **per-request state pool** for recurrent layers, with state snapshots attached to radix-tree nodes for prefix reuse (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6.3) — is SSD's chunk boundary, promoted to a cache architecture. The duality also normalised *talking* about hybrids: once an SSM layer and an attention layer are two computations of near-dual objects, interleaving them in one stack (and scheduling them in one engine) stops looking like a hack.
- **What it did not change:** the pure-vs-hybrid verdict. Mamba-2's own §9.2.3 is among the strongest published evidence *for* hybrids (above, §4), and no flagship pure-Mamba-2 language model followed. SSD narrowed the recall gap with larger state; it did not repeal the fixed-state copying bound (Jelassi et al., cited in `papers/mamba.md` §5.2), and its authors' production-facing successors all kept attention layers.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) Ollama / runners — serving an SSD-lineage model

- **Two state objects, two pools — budget both, from metadata.** A hybrid in this lineage presents a runner with (i) KV for its attention layers only — priced per token by $2 \times L_{\text{attn}} \times H_{kv} \times D \times b$, exactly as in `papers/gqa.md` §3 applied to the attention subset — and (ii) a **constant-size recurrent state per sequence**: per recurrent layer, (heads $\times P \times N$) state elements plus a conv state of (channels $\times$ kernel width). $N$ is the number to read carefully: Mamba-2-lineage models run $N$ = 64–128 as standard (Qwen3-Next's Gated DeltaNet uses head state dimension 128 in its published configuration summaries), i.e. **4–8$\times$ Mamba-1's per-layer state**, and the state is commonly kept in **fp32** in reference implementations even when weights and KV are quantised, because it is accumulated over the whole sequence. "Constant-size" at $N = 128$ across dozens of recurrent layers is how the corpus's 27B hybrid arrives at **≈158.9 MB per full snapshot** (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6.2) — a runner must surface state bytes per sequence as a first-class budget line beside KV bytes per token, or its fit arithmetic is wrong by that amount per concurrent session.
- **Prefix caching is checkpointing, and SSD tells you where the checkpoints can go.** Because the recurrent state is only *well-defined at chunk boundaries as a resumable object* in exactly the way it is at any token boundary — the state is the state — reuse remains whole-state snapshot/restore (see `papers/mamba.md` §6(a)). SSD adds one practical refinement: chunked prefill already materialises clean state at regular intervals, so a runner that snapshots at chunk boundaries pays almost nothing extra to create reuse points during a prefill it was doing anyway. The policy questions — how many snapshots, at which boundaries, evicted how — are the KV dossier's §6.4 questions, and SGLang's answers (snapshots as radix-node annotations, a cap per root-to-leaf path, a separate LRU for checkpoints) are the production reference.
- **Decode is the easy part; prefill shape is the design part.** Decode against resident SSD state is $O(1)$ per token and uniform across sequences — ideal for continuous batching. Prefill should be **chunked** (the §3.3 algorithm) rather than token-by-token recurrent: it uses tensor cores, bounds activation memory by chunk size, and interleaves fairly with other sequences' decode. A runner that implements Mamba-2 prefill as a naive loop has re-created the Mamba-1 scan problem one level up.
- **Portability warning, sharper than Mamba-1's.** Reference Mamba-2 speed assumes Triton/CUDA SSD kernels. On CPU, ARM, Metal or Vulkan targets (Ollama's and Aesir's home ground) the chunked form is actually *good news* relative to Mamba-1: intra-chunk work is ordinary matmul, which every backend already does well, and the inter-chunk recurrence is small. The risk is the opposite implementation error — a backend that falls back to a fully recurrent prefill because no chunked kernel was written for it, silently losing the architecture's main advance.

### (b) Aesir — its qwen35 work as the lived example

Reference, not re-derivation: `aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §3.2; `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6.2.

- Aesir's qwen35 27B is an SSD-lineage hybrid in the sense of §5: **64 blocks, most of them Gated-DeltaNet recurrent layers (SSD's gated scalar-decay state, delta-rule writes), full attention at intervals** (24 query / 4 KV heads, head dim 256, partial rotary 64/256), context 262,144. Everything §6(a) says about two pools is Aesir's daily arithmetic: KV priced only on attention layers (4,096 B/token each), state priced as a constant per sequence.
- **Q35-5 is the checkpointing discipline of §6(a), proven bit-exact:** snapshot (SSM + conv + boundary KV) at a prefix boundary, restore with **diffs 0.0** on all three components, **72 of 90 tokens reused** on a stateless resend after residency was destroyed, store **bounded at 4 snapshots per session (FIFO)** at **≈158.9 MB SSM+conv per snapshot**. The Mamba-2 dossier's contribution to reading that result: the snapshot *is* the SSD state at a boundary — there is no smaller or partial object that resumes the same computation — which is why the bound and the bit-exactness test, not clever slicing, are the whole design.
- The open Aesir work the KV dossier names (§6.4, Applicability (b)) sits exactly on this paper's seams: snapshot-placement policy (which chunk/prefix boundaries earn a 158.9 MB checkpoint), per-path caps, and two-list eviction — the policy layer SGLang built around the same mechanism. Mamba-2 itself contributes the placement hint: boundaries already materialised by chunked prefill are the cheap candidates.

## 7. Cross-links

- **KV-design dossier** (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §6): SSD-lineage state is that dossier's "hybrid complication" in its modern form. Where Mamba-1 made state cheap and small, Mamba-2 made it *larger and fp32-heavy by design* — the complication grew with the architecture's quality, and §6.2's 158.9 MB snapshot is a Mamba-2-lineage (Gated DeltaNet) state, not a Mamba-1 state.
- **Aesir ideas dossier** (`aesir/aesir-ideas-for-ollama.md` §3.2): Q35-5's bit-exact hybrid snapshots are the corpus's proof that SSD-state checkpointing works as a reuse primitive; this dossier is the architectural reason the checkpointed object has the shape and size it does.
- **GQA / MLA dossier** (`papers/gqa.md`, `papers/deepseek-v2-mla.md`) — **the three architectural responses to the same KV pressure, compared:** GQA cuts KV per token by head-sharing (exact, still growing); MLA cuts it by latent compression (token-addressable, still growing, reconstruction absorbed into the kernel); SSD/Mamba-2 abolishes per-token storage for its layers in favour of a fixed state — but note how Mamba-2 *moves toward* the other two in doing so: it borrows their vocabulary outright (heads, grouped-value sharing of $B$/$C$ in the pattern of GQA/MQA), enlarges the state until the fixed memory is no longer tiny, and its own best configuration (§4, hybrid experiments) reinstates a minority of exactly the attention layers GQA and MLA exist to cheapen. The three lines are converging on one design space — *how much exact, addressable memory does a layer need, and what do you pay for it?* — from three different starting points.
