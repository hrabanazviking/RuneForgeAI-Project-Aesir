# LongRoPE — LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens

## 1. Full citation

- **Title:** LongRoPE: Extending LLM Context Window Beyond 2 Million Tokens

- **Affiliation (as listed in the paper):** Microsoft Research
- **Venue / year:** Proceedings of the 41st International Conference on Machine Learning (ICML 2024), PMLR 235:11091–11104

## 2. The problem as framed

By early 2024 the RoPE-extension field (PI, NTK-aware, YaRN — `papers/yarn.md`) had converged on a practical ceiling around **128k tokens**. LongRoPE's paper frames three obstacles to going further, and they are worth keeping separate because each one defeats a different naive plan:

1. **Catastrophic new positions.** Extending 4k → 1000k+ means **more than 90% of positions are ones the model never saw in training**. Interpolating them into the trained range is the standard fix, but at a 512× extension ratio the interpolation itself becomes the damage: positions inside the *original* window are crushed into a tiny fraction of their trained angle range, and fine-tuning struggles to converge from so lossy a starting point.
2. **There is no data at the target length.** Texts longer than 1000k are scarce in every corpus, and fine-tuning directly at such lengths is computationally prohibitive even where data exists. Any method that requires training *at* 2048k in order to claim 2048k is dead on arrival.
3. **Short context pays for long context.** As the window grows, attention disperses across vastly more positions, and performance *inside the original window* degrades — the extended model gets worse at the lengths it was originally good at. An extension that costs the base capability is a trade most deployments cannot accept silently.

Underneath all three sits the paper's diagnostic claim: existing interpolation methods are **hand-designed rules imposed on a system that is not uniform**. PI uses one factor for everything; NTK spreads factors by a formula; YaRN sorts dimensions into three frequency bands with two hand-tuned boundaries (α = 1, β = 32 for Llama). The paper's motivating experiments (its Tables 1–3) show that a *searched* per-dimension rescaling beats every one of these rules at 8k–64k **without any fine-tuning** — e.g. on PG19 at 16k, searched factors give perplexity **11.34** against PI's 20.49, Dynamic-NTK's 23.29 and YaRN's 87.89 (YaRN, a method designed around fine-tuning, collapses here precisely because none was done; see §4). If the rules are leaving that much on the table at 16k, the paper argues, the right move at 2048k is not a better rule but a **search** — and a training schedule that never has to touch 2048k data.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

LongRoPE has three components: a searched, non-uniform rescaling; a progressive fine-tuning schedule that bootstraps it; and a recovery search that buys back short-context performance. The architecture, weights format and attention implementation are untouched — "models extended via LongRoPE retain the original architecture with minor modifications to the positional embedding, and can reuse most pre-existing optimizations" (abstract).

### 3.1 Two non-uniformities, searched per dimension and per position

The method searches two families of parameters jointly:

- **Per-dimension rescale factors *λ_i*.** Where PI divides every RoPE dimension's angle by the same *s*, and YaRN picks one of three treatments per dimension, LongRoPE assigns **each of the *d*/2 RoPE dimensions its own factor** *λ_i*, applied as angle *→ angle / λ_i*. *λ_i* = 1 is pure extrapolation for that dimension; *λ_i* = *s* is full PI; values in between — and, notably, up to *1.25·s*, slightly *more* interpolation than PI — are all in play, per dimension.
- **An initial-token threshold *n̂*.** The first *n̂* tokens receive **no interpolation at all** (factor 1 in every dimension); rescaling applies only from position *n̂* onward. The motivation is the attention-sink observation (the paper cites StreamingLLM and LM-Infinite): initial tokens attract disproportionate attention and anchor the attention distribution, so distorting their positions is disproportionately costly. The paper's ablation shows the optimal *n̂* is target-length-dependent (small values help at 8k/16k; the benefit fades at 2048k — the authors leave that as future work), which is exactly why it is a searched parameter rather than a constant.

The search space is deliberately huge — *λ_i* ∈ [1.0, 1.25·s] in steps of 0.01 for each dimension, *n̂* ∈ {0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 64, 128, 256}; at *s* = 4 the paper counts ~4×10¹⁶⁷ combinations — so the search is **evolutionary**, with two efficiency devices that are the real engineering content:

- **Seeded initial population.** The starting population of *P* candidates is not random: the PI, NTK and YaRN rescalings are inserted as individuals, and the remaining *P* − 3 are mutations of them. The search starts from the best hand-designed answers and improves them, rather than rediscovering them.
- **Monotonicity constraint.** Only candidates with *λ_i* non-decreasing in *i* are ever evaluated (lower dimensions = higher frequencies get less interpolation, per NTK theory). This prunes the space to physically plausible rescalings and is what makes perplexity-guided search affordable at large target lengths, where each fitness evaluation is a long-context forward pass.

Fitness is perplexity on a handful of long validation documents (5 random PG19 validation samples for targets ≤ 256k; 3 Books3 samples above 512k, with population and mutation/crossover sizes halved). Production search settings as reported: *P* = 64, mutation size *N₁* = crossover size *N₂* = 16, mutation probability *p* = 0.3, *𝒯* = 40 iterations, top-32 parents carried forward. The headline property the search buys *before any fine-tuning*: an **8× extension (LLaMA2 4k → 32k) with perplexity holding where PI, NTK and YaRN spike after 2×** (paper's Figure 3) — the searched rescaling preserves enough of the original RoPE's information to serve as a far better fine-tuning initialisation than any rule.

### 3.2 Progressive extension: fine-tune to 256k, search to 2048k

The 2048k result is a **two-stage bootstrap**, never a direct assault:

1. **Search** rescale factors for 128k and 256k targets on the *pre-trained* model (extension ratios 32× and 64× over LLaMA2's 4k).
2. **Fine-tune to 256k in two hops**: 400 steps with the 128k factors, then swap in the 256k factors on that checkpoint and fine-tune 600 more steps — **1,000 steps total, all at training lengths within 256k**. (LLaMA2 recipe as reported: learning rate 2×10⁻⁵ with linear decay, global batch 32, RedPajama chunked into 128k segments; 8× A100 at 128k, 16× A100 at 256k. Mistral recipe: constant 1×10⁻⁶, batch 64, 400 steps on the Long-Data Collections at 16k sequence length on 4× A100, following YaRN's setting — a detail that matters in §4.)
3. **Search again, on the fine-tuned 256k model**, for 2048k factors — and stop. The second jump (256k → 2048k) is another 8×, inside the range the search demonstrably handles *without* fine-tuning, so **no training at lengths above 256k ever occurs**. Total extension: 512× over LLaMA2's original window, 256× over Mistral's 8k.

### 3.3 Short-context recovery: a separate rescaling for short inputs

A 512× interpolation crushes the original 4k window's positions into 1/512 of their trained angular span, and the paper measures the damage (§4). The fix is a **third search, run at 4k/8k targets on the already-extended model**, with the maximum allowed *λ* reduced to encourage minimal interpolation at short lengths. At inference the model **switches factor sets by input length**: sequences under 8k use the recovery factors; longer sequences use the long factors. Two consequences a runner engineer must not miss: the "model" is really *two* RoPE configurations with a length-triggered switch between them, and the switch point makes RoPE configuration a per-request, length-dependent quantity — with the same cache-validity consequence as Dynamic-YaRN (a prefix computed under one factor set is not valid under the other; cf. `papers/yarn.md` §3.3 and `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §3.4).

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All figures are the paper's own measurements. Base models: **LLaMA2-7B (4k trained)** and **Mistral-7B v0.1 (8k trained)**. Perplexity is sliding-window (*S* = 256) unless noted; passkey retrieval uses **10 iterations** with the passkey at a uniformly random position — a small sample, and the accuracies below should be read with that grain size (±10 points is one iteration).

- **Non-fine-tuned extension (the search alone).** LLaMA2-7B, PG19 perplexity: searched dimension-wise factors **9.37 @ 8k / 11.34 @ 16k**, vs PI 10.65 / 20.49, Dynamic-NTK 10.21 / 23.29, YaRN 32.64 / 87.89. On Proof-pile the same comparison at 8k/16k: searched **3.45 / 3.13**, essentially tied with YaRN's 3.49 / 3.25 — i.e. the search's advantage is dataset-dependent at small extensions and decisive on PG19. At 64k (Proof-pile), searched factors give **3.22 before fine-tuning and 2.36 after**, vs YaRN 4.15 / 2.42 and PI 72.54 / 2.44: the searched initialisation wins before training and keeps a small lead after it.
- **Perplexity to 256k (Proof-pile, Table 5).** LongRoPE-2048k (fine-tuned to 256k) on LLaMA2-7B: **3.85 @ 4k, 3.65 @ 8k, 2.63 @ 32k, 2.38 @ 64k, 2.26 @ 128k, 1.87 @ 262k** — perplexity still *decreasing* at a quarter-million tokens, and better at every length than the YaRN *s* = 32 model (3.75 / 3.56 / 2.70 / 2.45 / 2.37 @ 128k, then **99.64 @ 262k** — past its extension, YaRN cliffs exactly as §2 of the YaRN dossier predicts). Note the honest wrinkle in the same table: at 4k the *unextended* LLaMA2 scores 3.58, better than any extended model — extension costs something at home lengths even here (see recovery, below).
- **Perplexity to 2048k (Books3, 20 books each > 2048k, sliding window 256k, Table 6).** LLaMA2 LongRoPE-2048k (ft = 256k): **6.81 @ 8k, 6.17 @ 256k, 6.17 @ 512k, 6.35 @ 1024k, 7.08 @ 2048k**. No cliff anywhere — but also no longer improving: the curve bottoms near 256k–512k and *rises* ~15% by 2048k. The ft = 128k variant is slightly better at the extreme end relative to its start (7.80 @ 2048k from 6.55 @ 8k is worse in absolute terms; the paper notes ft = 256k wins on LLaMA2 because its secondary ratio is 8× rather than 16×). Mistral tells the cautionary version: LongRoPE-2048k (ft = 128k) reaches **12.78 @ 2048k** (from 6.64 @ 8k) — it *functions* at 2048k where every baseline is `>10⁴`, but at nearly double its short-length perplexity. The paper attributes Mistral's weaker extreme-length behaviour to its fine-tune having used only 16k training sequences (§3.2).
- **Passkey retrieval (Figure 4, 10 iterations).** LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k (ft = 256k) maintains **≥ 90% accuracy from 4k to 2048k**; LongRoPE-Mistral-2048k (ft = 128k) holds **100% to 1800k, dropping to 60% at 2048k**. Every baseline (Together-32k, LongLoRA-100k, Code Llama, YaRN models) falls to ~0 beyond 128k. This is a single-fact retrieval probe — the easiest possible long-context task — and it is the *only* task the paper runs at million-token lengths.
- **Short-context benchmarks (HF Open LLM Leaderboard, Table 8).** LLaMA2-7B original **53.1 / 78.6 / 46.6 / 39.0** (ARC-c / HellaSwag / MMLU / TruthfulQA) → LongRoPE-2048k (ft = 128k) **52.9 / 76.5 / 43.4 / 38.8** → (ft = 256k) **51.0 / 75.3 / 39.6 / 37.3**: the longer fine-tune costs up to **7 MMLU points**. Mistral holds up better: original **60.6 / 83.2 / 63.6 / 42.6** → (ft = 128k) **59.0 / 81.2 / 61.3 / 43.1**. "Maintaining performance at the original short context window" (abstract) is thus true *with* the recovery search and truest for the 128k-fine-tuned variants; it is not costless.
- **Recovery ablation (Table 10).** Without recovery, LongRoPE-LLaMA2-2048k (ft = 128k) scores Proof-pile perplexity **4.16 @ 4k** and average benchmark accuracy **49.3**; with the §3.3 recovery search, **3.71 @ 4k** and **52.9**. The recovery step is not garnish — it is worth ~3.6 average benchmark points.
- **Second-interpolation ablation (Table 9, Books3).** Extending the same fine-tuned LLaMA2-256k checkpoint to 2048k by different methods: LongRoPE search **6.17 @ 512k / 6.35 @ 1024k / 7.08 @ 2048k**, vs YaRN 6.39 / 6.79 / 8.27 and PI 6.60 / 8.73 / 20.17. The searched factors' margin *grows* with extension ratio — the paper's core claim in miniature.
- **What the paper does not show:** no evaluation of any kind beyond perplexity and single-passkey retrieval at lengths above 256k; no multi-needle, aggregation, summarisation or QA task at extreme lengths; no latency or memory measurements (a 2048k window's KV cost is never mentioned); passkey *n* = 10. The 2048k claim is, precisely, "perplexity does not cliff and a single hidden number can usually be retrieved at 2048k, in models fine-tuned to 256k." Later, harder benchmarks (§5) showed how much weaker than "usable 2048k context" that is.

## 5. What it changed in practice

- **Phi-3: the documented production deployment.** The Phi-3 technical report states it directly: phi-3-mini ships with a default 4K context, and "we also introduce a long context version via LongRoPE that extends the context length to 128K, called phi-3-mini-128k." The report's account of the recipe is more than the paper's search alone — two stages of *long-context mid-training* plus *long-short mixed post-training* (SFT and DPO over both lengths), with the 128K model's quality reported on par with the 4K version. The same LongRoPE route produced the wider Phi-3 128k family (mini / small / medium 128k-instruct variants). What ships in the artifact is exactly §3's output in config form: the HF configuration for Phi-3-128k models carries `rope_scaling` with `type: "longrope"` and two explicit per-dimension arrays, **`short_factor` and `long_factor`** (one factor per RoPE dimension pair, length = hidden size ÷ heads ÷ 2), selected by whether the sequence is within or beyond `original_max_position_embeddings` (4096) — the paper's recovery/long factor-set switch, frozen into the model card. Hugging Face Transformers accordingly lists `"longrope"` as a first-class RoPE type alongside `"yarn"`, documented as "LongRoPE scaling as in Phi-3 model series," with `attention_factor` and the short/long factor arrays as its parameters.
- **What did *not* ship: a 2048k product, and a LongRoPE flag in local runners.** No released model serves the paper's 2048k configuration; the number lives in the paper and its released research checkpoints. And unlike YaRN, LongRoPE never became a user-facing scaling option in the llama.cpp/Ollama class of runners: there is no `--rope-scaling longrope` workflow for arbitrary models, because the method's output is a *searched, per-model factor table*, not a formula a runner can synthesise from a scale factor. In the local ecosystem LongRoPE arrives **baked into Phi-3 GGUF metadata** — the runner's obligation is the negative one: implement the factor arrays and the short/long switch *faithfully*, or the model's 128k claim is void on that runner. A runner that silently treats a LongRoPE model as unscaled RoPE (or as YaRN) will appear to work at 4k and degrade at length, with the blame landing on the model.
- **The successor's verdict — LongRoPE2 (ICML 2025), cited here as follow-on evidence, not as this paper's result.** Microsoft's own follow-up re-examined YaRN, NTK and LongRoPE extensions of Phi-3-mini to 128k on the RULER long-context benchmark and on short tasks: all three lost substantial MMLU points (YaRN −7.56, NTK −4.34, **LongRoPE −3.52**) and their *effective* context on RULER fell well short of the claimed 128k. LongRoPE2 exists precisely to close that gap (needle-driven search, mixed-context training), reporting near-lossless 128k extension of LLaMA3-8B. The lesson for dossier readers: the original LongRoPE paper's perplexity + passkey evidence *overstated* usable length even for its own production use case, by the producing lab's own later measurement. The 2048k headline should be filed accordingly — a genuine demonstration that positional collapse can be postponed by two orders of magnitude, not a demonstration of 2048k of usable attention.
- **Methodological legacy.** Independent of the headline number, two of LongRoPE's moves entered the standard toolkit: (i) *searched, per-dimension* rescaling as the quality ceiling that analytic rules (YaRN's bands) approximate — modern extension work reports searched factors or fits rules to them; and (ii) *progressive* extension — fine-tune at the longest length you can afford, then re-interpolate from there — which is now the default shape of long-context post-training schedules, including in staged form in Llama 3.1's 8k → 128k continued pre-training (a much larger token budget over the same idea; see `papers/yarn.md` §5).

## 6. Applicability

### (a) Ollama on a 6 GB consumer GPU — RoPE scaling trades quality for reach

- **LongRoPE is the limiting case of the corpus's central honesty problem, so do the arithmetic out loud.** The paper never prices its window; the KV formula does (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §1.2). At LLaMA2-7B's MHA rate of **512 KiB/token** (f16 cache), a 2048k window is **2,097,152 × 524,288 B = 1 TiB of KV cache** — before weights. Even at a modern GQA rate (Llama-3.1-8B class, **128 KiB/token**), 2048k is **256 GiB**, and Phi-3's actually-shipped 128k on a 6 GB card is already a negotiation among weights (~2–4 GB quantised), cache precision and window. **RoPE scaling decides whether positions are *meaningful*; it does not decide whether they are *affordable*, and it never reduces the price.** Any runner UI that lets a scaling method's headline number (2048k) anywhere near a context-length control on consumer hardware is lying by juxtaposition. The honest display is the trio from the YaRN dossier (`papers/yarn.md` §6a): trained length, scaled length *with its method and evidence class*, and allocated length *with its computed cache price*.
- **What a local runner can and cannot do with LongRoPE.** It *can*: load Phi-3-class models whose factor tables ship in their metadata, apply short/long factor sets on the correct side of `original_max_position_embeddings`, and key prefix caches by factor set (§3.3's switch invalidates cross-boundary reuse). It *cannot*: offer "LongRoPE" as a toggle for other models. The factors are the output of a GPU-days evolutionary search against a specific checkpoint's perplexity; there is no closed form to apply at load time, and presenting YaRN's formula under LongRoPE's name — or a generic per-dimension guess — would be fabrication. Where a user asks to push a non-LongRoPE model past its length, the runner's honest options remain YaRN (fine-tuned models; dynamic ~2× otherwise) or windowing (§7), labelled as such.
- **The short/long switch is a session-design hazard.** A Phi-3-128k chat that crosses 4,096 tokens changes RoPE configuration mid-conversation. If the runner recomputes the prefix under the new factors, the persona at the head of the chat is re-encoded — cheap insurance is to decide the factor set from the *session's allocated window*, not the current length, so a session lives under one configuration for its whole life. If it instead switches live, cached prefix blocks from the short regime must not be reused in the long regime (position-binding, `memory/kv-cache-design.md` §3.4). Ollama's current design — scaling as runner-load identity (`runners/ollama.md` §3.4) — accidentally enforces the safe version of this; a fork adding dynamic switching must re-derive the safety deliberately.
- **Guidance to surface to users, in the runner's voice:** for the models where LongRoPE is real (Phi-3 128k family), the claim to repeat is the *shipped* one — 128k, mid-trained and mixed-post-trained for it, quality reported on par at 4k by its maker — not the paper's 2048k research number, which belongs to different checkpoints (LLaMA2/Mistral research extensions) that no local user is running. Conflating a method's research maximum with a product's validated length is the single most common long-context dishonesty in the ecosystem; LongRoPE, uniquely, provides both numbers for the same method, which makes it the perfect teaching case.

### (b) Project Aesir (Mojo runner — sessions, prefix memory, hybrid SSM models)

- **Faithful LongRoPE support is a conformance requirement, not a feature.** Aesir must parse and apply per-dimension factor arrays (`short_factor` / `long_factor`) and the length-based switch exactly as the model config specifies, for every RoPE dimension pairing its kernels use — and prove it with the StreamingLLM-style discipline: run a Phi-3-128k checkpoint at lengths straddling 4,096 and at 32k–128k, and show perplexity continuity across the switch. A factor-table bug is silent at short lengths and corrosive at long ones; this is precisely the defect class Aesir's "verify from disk, grade by measurement" rules exist to catch.
- **The search itself is an Aesir-shaped tool.** If Aesir ever extends a house model, LongRoPE's recipe is the quality ceiling and its costs are now known quantities: perplexity-guided evolutionary search (population 64, 40 iterations, each fitness eval a long-document forward pass) plus ≤1k fine-tuning steps at the intermediate length on 8–16 A100-class devices. That is a *campaign*, not a load-time operation — budget it like the MimirAlloc campaigns, with the searched factors banked as versioned artifacts beside the model, checksummed like any other scarce asset. The cheaper analytic default remains YaRN (`papers/yarn.md` §6b); LongRoPE is what you commission when YaRN's bands measurably leave quality on the table for a specific checkpoint.
- **Progressive extension generalises to Aesir's session story.** The paper's bootstrap — master length *X*, then re-interpolate to 8*X* from the checkpoint that mastered *X* — is the same shape as Aesir's prefix-snapshot discipline: never leap more than one proven stage. For serving, the analogue is a *staged context policy*: a session earns larger windows by the runner verifying quality (needle probes at the session's actual lengths, run at admission) rather than by a config number — RULER's lesson (§5) is that claimed length and effective length diverge, and a runner that probes can report the effective one.
- **Hybrid SSM models:** LongRoPE's factors apply only to attention layers, and its search objective (whole-model perplexity) would silently optimise attention-layer factors against an SSM-state bottleneck it cannot see. If Aesir searches factors for a hybrid, the fitness function must include probes that separate the two failure modes (retrieval at distance, which attention carries, vs state-carried summarisation), or the search will "fix" the wrong subsystem. The paper's Mistral anomaly — same method, much worse extreme-length result, traced to a 16k fine-tuning sequence length — is the standing warning that in a staged recipe, the *weakest stage's* parameters govern the final claim.

## 7. Cross-links

- **KV-design dossier** (`memory/kv-cache-design.md`) — §1.2/§1.3 supply the arithmetic §6a uses to price LongRoPE's headline (512 KiB/token MHA → 1 TiB at 2048k; 128 KiB/token GQA → 256 GiB): the paper's silence on memory is filled by the corpus's formula, and the conclusion is that extreme scaling is a datacentre-research result, not a serving configuration. §3.4 (position-binding) governs the short/long factor switch: cached keys are valid only under the factor set that produced them.
- **Ollama dossier** (`runners/ollama.md`) — §4.1 (`num_ctx` is an allocation, clamped at GGUF trained length, with VRAM-tier defaults of 4,096 on small cards): the distance between Ollama's shipped default window and LongRoPE's research window is the whole consumer long-context story in one comparison. §3.4 (load-time options are runner identity) is why baked-in factor tables are safe in Ollama's model while any future dynamic factor switching would not be, without redesign. §4.5 (KV quantisation) lists the only levers that actually reduce the §6a price.
- **StreamingLLM dossier** (`papers/streamingllm.md`) — the explicit contrast this wave requires. **LongRoPE (and YaRN) scale positions so that a huge window remains fully addressable**: every token stays in the cache, attention stays exact, memory grows linearly without bound, and quality degrades *gradually and invisibly* as positional fidelity thins past the fine-tuned length. **StreamingLLM bounds the window and pins the anchor**: memory is constant, every attended position is at a perfectly trained distance, and what is lost is lost *entirely* — an evicted token is unrecoverable, but nothing in the window is half-remembered. For a long character chat the failure modes feel opposite: scaling gives you a character who was *present* for everything but recalls the distant past increasingly vaguely (and pays gigabytes for the privilege); window+anchor gives you a character with perfect recall of the recent scene and total amnesia beyond it, at a price a 6 GB card can pay forever. Note also the genuine point of contact: LongRoPE's searched *n̂* exemption for initial tokens is the attention-sink phenomenon arriving from the opposite direction — both papers conclude the first tokens' positions are load-bearing and must not be treated like the rest. The production answer composes them (scaled model for reach, sink+window for the chat that outruns even the scaled window, external memory for facts), and any runner presenting either method alone as "infinite context" is misdescribing it.
- **YaRN dossier** (`papers/yarn.md`) — the predecessor and the foil: analytic three-band rule vs searched per-dimension factors; fine-tune-first evidence vs search-first evidence; the method that shipped as everyone's config block vs the method that shipped inside one model family. LongRoPE's Tables 1–3 are also the corpus's sharpest evidence about YaRN's limits without fine-tuning — read the two dossiers as one argument.
- **GQA dossier** (`papers/gqa.md`) — as with YaRN: the KV-head count of the base model, not the scaling method, sets whether a given extended length costs 256 GiB or 1 TiB. Extension headlines that do not name the base model's cache rate are incomplete claims.
