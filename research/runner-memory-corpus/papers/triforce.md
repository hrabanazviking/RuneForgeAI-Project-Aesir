## 2. The problem as framed

Speculative decoding's standard bargain — a cheap draft proposes, the expensive target verifies in parallel, rejection sampling keeps the output distribution exact — quietly assumes the draft is cheap. **At long context that assumption fails twice, and the second failure is the paper's subject.**

The first failure is familiar: autoregressive decoding loads the model weights *and the entire KV cache* for every generated token. The KV cache grows linearly with sequence length, so past some length it stops being an accessory to the weights and becomes the dominant load — the paper's Figure 2 makes the crossover concrete for its running example: Llama2-7B-128K carries **14 GB of weights against a 64 GB KV cache at full context** (project-page figures, consistent with the paper's framing). Every token's latency is then set mostly by dragging cache, not weights, through memory.

The second failure is the one that kills naive speculation at long context. A draft model for a 128K-context target must itself *read* a long context to draft well. The options are all bad, and the paper measures why:

- **Train a small long-context drafter:** training draft models to match a ~128K–1M context target is expensive in its own right, and it is unclear a small model can hold accuracy at those lengths at all.
- **Give an existing small drafter a compressed cache** (the training-free route — StreamingLLM or H2O eviction on the draft side): the paper's Figure 2(a) shows the draft/target divergence **growing continuously with sequence length** under both eviction schemes — acceptance decays as generation proceeds, so the speedup speculation was bought for evaporates exactly where the context is longest. Eviction is also *lossy in a way speculation cannot repair on the draft side*: an evicted KV pair is gone, and if the future text needs it — the needle-retrieval case — the draft simply never proposes the right token. The paper's Table 1 quantifies the collapse: on a needle task at 120K context with a 4K cache budget, acceptance falls to **0.052 (StreamingLLM)** and **0.074 (H2O)**, against 0.916 / 0.918 for the same methods on PG-19 prose. Eviction-based drafting works until the task actually needs the long context, which is the only reason the long context exists.

So the problem as framed: **at long context the draft model drowns in KV too — speculation's speedup dies unless the draft's context is cheapened as well — but cheapening it by eviction destroys the acceptance rate (and, on retrieval-heavy text, the draft's usefulness entirely).** What is needed is a draft whose context is cheap *and* whose errors remain correctable — i.e. cheapened by **selection against a retained full cache**, not by deletion, inside a speculation scheme whose final verification still sees everything.

The paper's three enabling observations (its §3): **(i) attention sparsity** — at 120K context, ~4K tokens recover over 96% of the attention score across almost all layers of Llama2-7B-128K, so a small *well-chosen* subset of the cache can draft with high acceptance (1K tokens give a theoretical 97.6% acceptance under oracle Top-K selection; practical selectors exceed 90.5% at 1K); **(ii) contextual locality** — adjacent decoding steps need largely the *same* subset, so a selected draft cache can be reused across many steps and its construction cost amortised; **(iii) dual bottlenecks** — weights and KV are *separate* bottlenecks that dominate in different regimes, so they can be attacked by *separate* speculation levels in sequence.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

TriForce is a **three-level hierarchy** — two models, three caches — in which the draft/verify split of ordinary speculation is applied recursively. Name the pieces as the paper does (its Figure 1 and Algorithm 1):

- **Target model `M_p` with full cache `C_p`** — the original model, full KV, the only authority. Nothing is ever evicted from `C_p`.
- **Retrieval draft: `M_p` itself, with retrieval cache `C_r`** — the *same weights* as the target, run against a small selected subset of the KV cache. This is **self-speculation**: the intermediate drafter differs from the target only in how much context it looks at.
- **Lightweight draft model `M_q` with StreamingLLM cache `C_q`** — a genuinely small model (JackFram/Llama-68M in the evaluation) holding only sink + recent tokens, drafting for the retrieval draft.

Generation proceeds as a **verification cascade** with two speculation lengths, `γ₁` (small model → retrieval draft) and `γ₂` (retrieval draft → target):

1. **Inner speculation (attacks the weights bottleneck).** `M_q`, cheap in both weights and cache, drafts `γ₁` tokens autoregressively. The retrieval draft `M_p(C_r)` verifies them in one parallel pass under standard speculative sampling, correcting the first rejection from the residual distribution. Repeat until `γ₂` tokens have been produced by the retrieval draft. The point: the retrieval draft still loads the *full model weights* per token it produces, so letting a 68M model propose its tokens converts most of those weight-loads into one batched verification.
2. **Outer verification (attacks the KV bottleneck).** The `γ₂` tokens from the retrieval draft are verified in one parallel pass by `M_p(C_p)` — the target with the **full** cache — again under standard speculative sampling, with correction on first rejection. Only tokens that survive this pass enter the output.
3. **Cache maintenance.** After each outer iteration, `C_r` and `C_q` are updated from the accepted tokens; `C_r` is rebuilt (see below) either at a fixed stride or when the rolling average acceptance rate drops below a threshold.

### Retrieval-based drafting — how the reduced cache is built (paper §4.1)

The retrieval cache is the paper's answer to §2's eviction failure, and its defining property is that **selection happens against a full cache that is never destroyed**:

- The full KV cache is segmented into **small chunks**. To build (or rebuild) `C_r`, the system scores each chunk by the attention between the current query — initially the last prefilled token — and the **mean key of the chunk**, ranks chunks by that score, and gathers the top chunks up to a fixed budget (4K tokens in the headline configuration — ≈3% of a 128K context) into `C_r`. Selection is by **relevance to the current query**, not by recency or cumulative-attention bookkeeping: a chunk from position 3,000 outranks a recent chunk whenever the query actually attends to it.
- Between rebuilds, contextual locality (§2, observation ii) is exploited directly: `C_r` is *reused* across decoding steps; as new tokens are generated, their KV entries overwrite the **least-important** entries in `C_r` rather than triggering a rebuild. Rebuilds happen on the stride/acceptance-drop triggers above. The construction cost is thus paid occasionally, not per token.
- The contrast the paper draws is exact: StreamingLLM and H2O **evict** — the discarded pair cannot be recovered, so a selection mistake is permanent and compounds; retrieval **selects per rebuild from the intact full cache**, so a chunk missed in one rebuild can be retrieved in the next, and the final verifier's view is never reduced at all. Table 1 is the evidence offered: at 120K context / 4K budget, retrieval drafting holds acceptance at **0.965 on PG-19 and 0.988 on needle retrieval**, against eviction's 0.916/0.052 and 0.918/0.074 — near the oracle Top-K reference (0.992 / 0.999) on both, where eviction collapses precisely on the task that needs the context.

One implementation honesty note the paper states itself: the sparsity analysis motivating the design exempts the first two layers (which are not sparse), but the shipped system **maintains sparsity at all layers** to keep the drafting cost constant under CUDA Graphs — accepting a somewhat lower acceptance rate as the price of a fixed-shape draft pass.

### The losslessness argument, as stated

TriForce's claim to "lossless" rests on the cascade's final step, and the paper states the argument in one sentence (§4.2): *the original output distribution is preserved during the final speculation phase, which is identical to the standard speculative decoding algorithm, and the proof is trivial.* Unpacked: every approximation in the system — the 68M model's StreamingLLM cache, the retrieval draft's 4K selected cache — lives strictly on the **proposal side** of the outer verification. Proposals may be arbitrarily bad; speculative sampling's accept/correct rule against `M_p(C_p)`'s true distributions guarantees the emitted tokens are distributed exactly as the full-cache target's own autoregressive output, whatever proposed them. The inner level is the same argument one level down (the retrieval draft's distribution is preserved against `M_q`'s proposals), which matters only for the *quality of proposals* reaching the outer level, not for final exactness. Lossiness is thereby confined to where it can only cost speed: a poor retrieval selection lowers acceptance, never correctness — the structural opposite of eviction-based *inference*, where the reduced cache **is** the model's view and its mistakes are emitted. (Whether a given *implementation* honours this argument is a separate question — see §6b for the corpus's rule.)

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with hardware context

All figures are the paper's own; nothing is re-derived. **Setup:** targets Llama2-7B-128K, Llama2-13B-128K, LWM-Text-Chat-128K (128K context window); retrieval-cache budget **4K**; initial draft model JackFram/Llama-68M with StreamingLLM cache; datasets PG-19 and NarrativeQA, 100 examples each; prompt length **122K** (on-chip) / **127K** (offloading), generation length 256 tokens; implementation on Hugging Face with CUDA Graphs and FlashAttention; offloading keeps weights on GPU and offloads overflow KV to CPU (AMD EPYC 9754 host).

**Headline (abstract):** up to **2.31×** speedup for Llama2-7B-128K on a single **A100** (on-chip). Offloading on **two RTX 4090s**: **0.108 s/token** for Llama2-7B-128K — **7.78×** over the authors' optimised offloading system, and characterised as only ~2× slower per token than the autoregressive baseline *on an A100*. On a **single RTX 4090**, **4.86×** over DeepSpeed-Zero-Inference.

**On-chip detail (paper Table 2, A100; prompt 122K, generation 256; speedup with mean acceptance rate in parentheses; baseline "naive policy" = JF68M + StreamingLLM cache drafting directly for the target):**

| T | TriForce | Naive policy |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | **2.31× (0.923)** | 1.56× (0.465) |
| 0.6 | 2.19× (0.914) | 1.42× (0.404) |
| 1.0 | 2.08× (0.900) | 1.29× (0.287) |
| 1.2 | 2.02× (0.890) | 1.27× (0.266) |

The hierarchy's margin over the naive policy *widens* with temperature, because the naive draft's acceptance collapses (0.465 → 0.266) while TriForce's holds above 0.89 throughout — the robustness claim in numeric form. Ablations at T=0.6 isolate the two levels: retrieval drafting **without** hierarchy 1.80× (acceptance 0.913 — the selection is doing the acceptance work), StreamingLLM drafting **with** hierarchy 1.90× (0.875), full TriForce 2.19× — both levels pay, and neither alone reaches the pair.

**Offloading detail (paper Table 3, T=0.6; latency per token):**

| GPUs | Target | TriForce | AR baseline | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2× RTX 4090 | Llama2-7B-128K | 108 ms | 840 ms | **7.78×** |
| 2× RTX 4090 | LWM-Text-Chat-128K | 114 ms | 840 ms | 7.37× |
| 2× RTX 4090 | Llama2-13B-128K | 226 ms | 1,794 ms | **7.94×** |
| 1× RTX 4090 | Llama2-7B-128K | 312 ms | 1,516 ms* | **4.86×** |
| 1× RTX 4090 | LWM-Text-Chat-128K | 314 ms | 1,516 ms* | 4.83× |

(*single-GPU baseline is DeepSpeed-Zero-Inference, per the paper's footnote.)

**Scaling, batching, and budget (as reported):** speedup *grows* with context length (paper Figure 5) — the draft/target latency gap widens as KV dominates, giving a stated **theoretical upper bound of 13.1×**, ~7× the naive policy's bound; batched inference on A100 reaches **1.90× at batch 6** (19K context per sample) and 1.61–1.89× across the batch/context grid of Table 4, ahead of the naive policy in every cell; the budget ablation (Figure 6a) shows acceptance rising with retrieval budget up to **4K and plateauing thereafter**, which is why 4K is the headline operating point — beyond it, drafting latency eats what acceptance gains. Chunk size cuts both ways as reported: too small overfits single tokens, too large lets high-score tokens be averaged away inside their chunk and starves selection diversity (at chunk 2K under a 4K budget only two chunks can be chosen).

**Compatibility note (paper §5.2.3):** TriForce is evaluated for composition with tree-based speculation (Sequoia trees and independent sequences), with the *theoretical* tokens-per-target-step improving under larger speculation budgets — reported as an estimate from acceptance statistics, not as an end-to-end tree implementation.

## 5. Position vs waves 2/13 + ledger #46

**Against wave 2 (foundations).** TriForce inherits speculative decoding's exactness contract whole and changes nothing about the accept/correct rule; its move is architectural — *apply the draft/verify split to the draft itself*. Where wave 2 asks "what small model can propose for this target?", TriForce asks "what can propose for a target whose cost is mostly cache?" and answers: *the target itself, looking at less cache* — self-speculation — with a conventional small model demoted to proposing for *that*. The draft model problem at long context (train a 128K small model, or watch an evicted small model's acceptance rot) is not solved but *dissolved*: the high-acceptance drafter needs no training because it is the target, and the trained small model that remains (68M) needs no long context because its cache is StreamingLLM's by design and its errors are corrected by a drafter that has the retrieval cache.

**Against wave 3 (KV selection) — the composition this wave exists to record.** The retrieval cache is wave 3's selection logic transplanted to the draft side. SnapKV selects prompt KV per head by observation-window attention votes, once, at the prefill boundary, and the *model itself* then runs on the selection — selection errors are output errors. TriForce's retrieval selects chunks by query-vs-mean-key attention, repeatedly, against a retained full cache, and only a *drafter* runs on the selection — selection errors are acceptance errors, corrected by full-cache verification. Same family of signal (attention mass predicts future need), opposite failure semantics. H2O and StreamingLLM appear in TriForce in both of their corpus roles at once: as the *foil* (eviction on the main draft path collapses on needle retrieval, Table 1) and as a *component* (StreamingLLM cache on the 68M model, where eviction is safe because two verification levels sit above it). The corpus's selection ideas thus compose with speculation in a precise sense: **any lossy KV scheme becomes lossless-by-construction when demoted to proposal duty under a full-cache verifier** — the guarantee comes from the cascade's top, not from the scheme.

**Against wave 13 (tree verification).** Orthogonal axis. SpecInfer/EAGLE-2 widen the *proposal* (many candidates per step, verified in parallel); TriForce deepens the *pipeline* (more levels of proposer, each verified by the next). Nothing in TriForce's hierarchy presumes or precludes trees — its own §5.2.3 treats Sequoia-style trees as an add-on that raises tokens-per-step within each level. Conversely, wave 13's papers are all short/medium-context evaluations; none of them prices what a verification pass costs when the context is 122K, which is the cost TriForce exists to avoid paying at draft time.

**Against IDEA-LEDGER #46 — the precise statement.** #46 records the small-card verdict: *tree verification pays at width ≈2–3 and low-tens total nodes (≈8–16), depth over breadth, budget measured locally* — derived in wave 13 for **short/medium context on a 6 GB-class card**, where the binding costs are per-node attention over a modest resident context, KV writes, and logits, all paid from one narrow bandwidth budget. TriForce's relation to that verdict, stated exactly:

- **TriForce does not address #46's question, and neither confirms nor refutes its numbers.** TriForce's speculation is *chain-shaped* at both levels (γ₁, γ₂ are lengths, not tree widths); it contains no tree-width experiment, on any hardware, at any context. #46's width verdict survives this paper untouched *inside its stated regime*.
- **TriForce bounds #46 by regime: it identifies the context length at which #46's cost model stops being the binding one.** #46 prices speculation as if the draft were cheap and verification width were the scarce spend. TriForce's measurements show that premise inverting as context grows: by 122K the *draft's own cache load* is a first-order cost, acceptance under cheap drafting collapses (naive policy: 0.465 at T=0, falling with length), and the winning spend is not width but a *second speculation level plus a selected draft cache*. In other words, #46 answers "how wide?" for the regime where drafting is nearly free; TriForce shows that at long context the prior question is "how does the draft afford to read?" — and a runner that carried #46's narrow-tree tuning unchanged into a 100K-context workload would have optimised the smaller term. The verdict is therefore **bounded, not overturned**: correct where derived (short/medium context, small card), incomplete as a total speculation policy once context reaches the tens-of-thousands and beyond.
- **The two compose, and the composition is the wave's joint claim.** Nothing prevents a narrow, value-ranked tree (per #46, sized by Sequoia's cost model — companion dossier §5) from being verified at TriForce's outer level, or from widening the inner level's proposals; TriForce's own tree-compatibility estimate points that way. #46 governs *width within a level*; TriForce governs *how many levels and what each level reads*. Refinement of the ledger entry is the coordinator's to bank; this dossier's statement is that the available refinement is a **scope bound** (long-context workloads add a draft-cache axis #46 does not price), with no change to #46's width numbers or their evidence grade.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) Personal long-context chat and the ggml-class stack

*[Engineering judgement, labelled as such; the paper's speedups are A100 / RTX 4090 numbers at 122K–127K prompts and are not imported as expectations.]*

Personal long-context chat is — honestly assessed — **the one local workload where TriForce's regime actually occurs**. The fleet's ordinary chat lives at short/medium context, where #46's world holds and TriForce buys nothing. But the ingestion-shaped use the user actually has — a very large document, character/world corpus, or accumulated history pushed toward 100K+ tokens, followed by long generation against it — reproduces the paper's operating point exactly: KV cache larger than the weights, per-token latency dominated by cache traffic, and a draft that would have to read the same mountain to propose anything. If any local workload justifies hierarchical speculation, it is that one, and no other paper in the corpus addresses its draft side.

The adoption price, however, is asymmetric against the companion paper, and the dossier should say so plainly:

- **Sequoia needs a cost model and a tree builder — host-side, cheap** (companion dossier §6a), *given* a tree-verification substrate.
- **TriForce needs a second, retrieval-KV draft path — substantial, in-engine work.** Concretely, a ggml-class stack would need: (i) the full KV cache retained in a form the retrieval scorer can read (chunk mean-keys materialised and maintained — a new cache-side data structure, per layer); (ii) a **gather path** that assembles a per-step selected subset cache `C_r` and runs the *target weights* against it as a separate draft graph — i.e. the engine must run the same model in two cache configurations concurrently, with two live KV views; (iii) the inner speculation loop (small model + StreamingLLM cache) nested inside the outer one, with acceptance-triggered `C_r` rebuilds and least-importance overwrites as cache-manager policy; (iv) all of it under the fixed-shape discipline (constant draft budget) that CUDA-Graph-style execution — and any graph-capturing ggml backend — requires. Items (i)–(ii) are the real cost: they are cache-architecture work of the same order as adopting paged attention, not sampler work. A realistic local verdict: **TriForce is a design to learn from now and a build only if long-context ingestion becomes a primary local workload**; its *retrieval-draft idea* has a cheaper partial form — a single speculation level in which the ordinary small draft model is replaced, for very long contexts only, by self-speculation against a SnapKV/retrieval-selected cache — which captures the acceptance-preservation point (Table 1's needle gap is the argument) without the inner 68M level. Even that partial form still needs (i)–(ii).

One further honesty constraint: TriForce's speedups are quoted at 122K–127K contexts on models *trained for* 128K. A local model whose effective context is far below its nominal window (see §7, RULER) would draft and verify fluently over context it does not actually use well — the acceleration would be real and the answers still limited by the base model. TriForce makes long generation *faster*; it cannot make it *better*.

### (b) The losslessness standard

TriForce's title claims "lossless," and §3 above states its argument as the paper gives it: exactness is inherited from the final full-cache speculative-sampling verification, with all approximation confined to proposal duty. **Under the corpus's grading discipline, that claim is not taken on trust — for this paper or any other.** "Lossless" is verified by raw-oracle comparison: the accelerated system's distributions are compared against the unaccelerated target as oracle (teacher-forced rank and logit-gap on the raw prompt, per the corpus's comparator discipline), and the claim is credited only where the comparison passes. This matters more for TriForce than for a plain chain, because its implementation surface for silent exactness bugs is larger: two nested accept/correct loops, a rebuilt draft cache whose staleness must affect *only* proposals, and an acceptance-triggered rebuild policy that must never leak into the full cache's contents. Each is a place where a real implementation can quietly become lossy (e.g. a `C_r` rebuild that mutates shared KV state, a correction step that resamples from the retrieval distribution instead of the full one) while still producing plausible text and good speed numbers. The paper's argument tells us the *design* is exact; the oracle comparison is what would tell us a *build* is. The dossier therefore records TriForce as **lossless as designed and as argued, unverified as implemented** — the same grade the corpus gives every distribution-faithfulness claim, Sequoia's included (companion dossier §6b).
