# YaRN — YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models

## 1. Full citation

- **Title:** YaRN: Efficient Context Window Extension of Large Language Models
- **Authors:** Bowen Peng, Jeffrey Quesnelle, Honglu Fan, Enrico Shippole
- **Affiliations (as listed in the paper):** Nous Research; EleutherAI; University of Geneva
- **Venue / year:** International Conference on Learning Representations (ICLR 2024), poster. arXiv v1 31 August 2023; the HTML text read for this dossier is v3.

## 2. The problem as framed

A model trained with Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE) knows positions only inside the window it was trained on — 2k tokens for LLaMA, 4k for Llama 2. Feed it position *L+1* and it is not merely uninformed, it is out of distribution: the rotation angles for the low-frequency RoPE dimensions have never been seen in those combinations, attention logits take values the softmax was never calibrated for, and perplexity does not slope upward — it falls off a cliff. The paper's own tables make the cliff concrete: an unextended Llama 2 model's sliding-window perplexity on long documents goes from single digits inside its window to `>10` and then `>10^4`-class values beyond it (the same cliff appears, tabulated by the LongRoPE paper, as `>10^4` for base LLaMA2-7B at every evaluation length from 8k upward — `papers/longrope.md` §4).

There are only two naive ways past the wall, and the paper frames both as failures:

- **Extrapolation** — change nothing and let positions run past *L*. This preserves local relative distances perfectly (nearby tokens keep exactly the geometry they were trained with) and fails globally, because the unseen angles are catastrophic. It is the zero-cost option and it collapses almost immediately beyond the trained length.
- **Interpolation** — Position Interpolation (PI, Chen et al. 2023, concurrent with kaiokendev) rescales every position *m → m/s*, where *s = L′/L* is the extension factor, so the longest new sequence fits back inside the trained angle range. Nothing is out of distribution any more, but every dimension is compressed by the same factor: adjacent tokens move *s×* closer together in *every* RoPE dimension, the high-frequency dimensions that carry fine local order lose the resolution the model relies on to tell neighbours apart, and the damage grows with *s*. The paper reports that PI fine-tunes in practice stalled around *s ≈ 8* before outputs degraded even after fine-tuning.

The design problem is therefore not "interpolate or extrapolate" but **which dimensions may be interpolated, which must be left alone, and what else breaks when you stretch** — plus an efficiency problem: PI-era extensions were expensive (the paper's costing table puts a PI extension to just 16k at 640 A100-hours and an NTK-aware Code Llama extension near 100k at 6,400 A100-hours; see §4). A method that needs a large fraction of pre-training to fix a positional encoding is not a practical extension method. YaRN's claim is to be the efficient, principled point in this space: state-of-the-art extension after fine-tuning on **less than ~0.1% of the original pre-training data**, with **10× fewer tokens and 2.5× fewer training steps than previous methods** (abstract, as reported).

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

YaRN is two ideas composed, both operating purely on how RoPE angles are computed. No architecture changes, no attention-code changes, and — because the modified quantities are the precomputed rotary embeddings — **zero added inference or training cost** over any other interpolation method, with direct compatibility with FlashAttention-2.

### 3.1 NTK-by-parts: interpolate per dimension, by wavelength

RoPE gives each dimension pair *d* its own rotation frequency *θ_d = b^(−2d/|D|)* (base *b = 10,000*) and hence its own **wavelength** *λ_d = 2π/θ_d* — the distance over which that dimension completes one full rotation. Dimensions differ enormously: high-frequency dimensions rotate many times inside the training window and can only encode *relative* position (a rotation that has wrapped 50 times cannot tell you where you are absolutely); low-frequency dimensions do not complete even one rotation inside the window, so every position has a unique angle in them and they carry *absolute* position information.

NTK-by-parts keys the treatment to the ratio ***r(d) = L / λ_d*** — how many rotations dimension *d* completes inside the original context *L*:

- ***r(d) < α* — interpolate fully.** These are the long-wavelength, absolute-position dimensions. Their angles *would* go out of distribution at longer lengths, so they are compressed exactly as in PI: *θ_d → θ_d / s*.
- ***r(d) > β* — do not interpolate at all.** These are the short-wavelength, relative-position dimensions. Their angles stay in-distribution at any length (they wrap), and compressing them would destroy local resolution for no benefit, so they are extrapolated untouched: *θ_d → θ_d*.
- ***α ≤ r(d) ≤ β* — blend.** A linear ramp *γ(r) = (r − α)/(β − α)* mixes the two: *h(θ_d) = (1 − γ)·θ_d/s + γ·θ_d*.

For the Llama family the paper's experimentally found values are ***α = 1, β = 32***. This is the disciplined version of the community "NTK-aware" idea (which spread interpolation pressure by changing the RoPE base — effective, but the right base had to be found empirically per target length): NTK-by-parts states the rule in terms of what each dimension *encodes*, with two interpretable boundary parameters instead of one opaque base change. In current serving configs these survive verbatim as YaRN's `beta_fast` (= *β* = 32) and `beta_slow` (= *α* = 1) parameters (§5).

### 3.2 Attention temperature: stretching raises entropy, so sharpen the logits

Interpolation has a second-order effect the earlier methods missed: as positions are compressed, query–key dot products spread differently and the attention distribution's entropy grows — attention gets flatter, less decisive. YaRN corrects this with a **temperature** on the attention logits:

```
softmax( q_mᵀ k_n / (t · √|D|) )
```

with the fitted, closed-form recommendation for Llama / Llama 2 models:

```
√(1/t)  =  0.1 · ln(s) + 1
```

fitted by sweeping for the lowest-perplexity temperature at each scale *s* on LLaMA 7B–65B without fine-tuning, and reported to transfer to Llama 2 (7B–70B) — the authors suggest the entropy effect has a degree of universality. The implementation trick matters for runner engineers: instead of touching the attention kernel, YaRN multiplies the (complex) rotary embeddings themselves by *√(1/t)* — a "length scaling" of both queries and keys — so the temperature is folded into quantities that are precomputed once and reused. In serving configs this is the `attention_factor` field; in llama.cpp it is derived from the scale when YaRN scaling is selected (§5).

### 3.3 Dynamic-YaRN: the no-fine-tuning variant

Static scaling fixes *s = L′/L* for a preset target length, which taxes sequences shorter than *L′* (they are processed with unnecessarily compressed positions) and still cliffs at *L′*. The paper's Dynamic Scaling alternative recomputes the effective scale per forward pass from the *current* length, *s = max(1, l′/L)*, degrading gracefully instead of breaking at the boundary. Combined with NTK-aware interpolation this is "Dynamic NTK"; combined with YaRN it is **Dynamic-YaRN**, which the paper reports allows **more than 2× extension with no fine-tuning at all**. One implementation warning in the paper is load-bearing for any runner that caches: under dynamic scaling every token's RoPE changes when *s* changes, so **the KV cache must store keys *before* RoPE is applied** (or be invalidated on scale change) — a cached post-RoPE key is silently wrong under a different scale. This is a special case of the corpus's position-binding rule (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §3.4).

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All figures are the paper's own measurements. Fine-tuning recipe, stated once because every headline number depends on it: Llama 2 7B and 13B; AdamW (β₁ = 0.9, β₂ = 0.95), learning rate 2×10⁻⁵, 20-step linear warmup, no weight decay; PyTorch FSDP + FlashAttention-2; training data is **PG19 chunked into 64k segments**. The *s = 16* models (4k → 64k) were fine-tuned for **400 steps at global batch size 64**. The *s = 32* models (4k → 128k) were **not** trained on 128k data: they start from the finished *s = 16* checkpoint and train **200 further steps, still on 64k data** — the paper's "train short, test long" demonstration. Ablations use LLaMA 7B (2k base) extended *s = 16* to 32k, 400 steps on 32k PG19 chunks.

- **Long-sequence perplexity (Proof-pile, sliding window *S = 256*, 10 documents of ≥128k tokens).** Llama 2-7B YaRN *s = 16*: **3.51 @ 8k, 2.99 @ 16k, 2.65 @ 32k, 2.42 @ 64k, >10 @ 128k** — it works to its fine-tuned length and fails beyond it, as expected. Llama 2-7B YaRN *s = 32*: **3.56, 3.04, 2.70, 2.45, 2.37 @ 128k** — perplexity keeps *falling* out to 128k despite never having seen a training sequence longer than 64k. The 13B pair mirrors this (*s = 32*: 3.29 @ 8k → **2.24 @ 128k**). Perplexity decreasing with evaluation length is the signature of a model genuinely using the longer context, not merely surviving it.
- **Head-to-head at equal budget (ablation, fine-tuned LLaMA 7B → 32k, 400 steps).** YaRN has the lowest training loss throughout (fastest convergence of PI, NTK-aware, NTK-by-parts and YaRN) and the lowest Proof-pile perplexity across lengths in the paper's Figure 3 / Appendix Table 5 comparison, in both fine-tuned and non-fine-tuned settings; the paper's passkey-retrieval evaluation (50 iterations, passkey at a uniformly random position, prompt lengths 2k–32k) likewise ranks YaRN highest among the four interpolation methods at the same training budget. The figure reports these graphically; the paper does not tabulate exact passkey percentages for this ablation, and this dossier does not invent them.
- **Short-context cost (HF Open LLM Leaderboard: 25-shot ARC-c, 10-shot HellaSwag, 5-shot MMLU, 0-shot TruthfulQA).** Llama 2-7B baseline **53.1 / 77.8 / 43.8 / 39.0** → YaRN *s = 16* **52.3 / 78.8 / 42.5 / 38.2** → YaRN *s = 32* **52.1 / 78.4 / 41.7 / 37.3**. Llama 2-13B baseline **59.4 / 82.1 / 55.8 / 37.4** → *s = 32* **58.0 / 82.2 / 51.9 / 37.3**. The paper calls this "minimal degradation" and notes an average ~0.49% score drop between the *s = 16* and *s = 32* models; read precisely, the cost is real, concentrated in MMLU (−2.1 points at 7B *s = 32*; −3.9 at 13B *s = 32*), and grows with the extension factor. Extension is not free even when it is cheap.
- **Efficiency (paper's Table 4, A100-hours).** YaRN: LLaMA 7B → 32k in **128 GPU-hours**; Llama 2 7B → 64k in **256**; → 128k in **256 + 128** (the two-stage recipe). Comparators as reported: PI → 16k in **640**; NTK-aware (Xiong et al.) → ~50k in **64,000**; Code Llama NTK-aware → ~100k in **6,400**. These are the source of the abstract's "10× less tokens, 2.5× less training steps" efficiency claim — a claim about the *fine-tune*, relative to prior extension methods, not about pre-training.
- **What the paper does not show:** no needle/RULER-style multi-fact retrieval suite (passkey is a single-fact probe), no evaluation beyond 128k, no human or downstream-task long-context evaluation, and perplexity is measured with a 256-token sliding window, which credits local fluency heavily. Later work (§5) found exactly the gap this leaves: models extended this way can hold perplexity and pass single-needle tests well past the length where multi-needle and aggregation tasks have already degraded.

## 5. What it changed in practice

YaRN is the rare community-origin method that became the **default scaling dialect of the serving stack**. Verified current public state, accessed 2026-11-15:

- **A first-class `rope_scaling` type everywhere.** Hugging Face Transformers lists `"yarn"` among its supported RoPE types, parameterised by exactly the paper's quantities: `factor` (= *s*), `original_max_position_embeddings` (= *L*), `beta_fast` (default 32 = *β*), `beta_slow` (default 1 = *α*), and `attention_factor` (the temperature term). vLLM and SGLang accept the same block (e.g. `--rope-scaling '{"rope_type":"yarn","factor":4.0,"original_max_position_embeddings":32768}'`). In llama.cpp / GGUF the method is selected with `--rope-scaling yarn --rope-scale <s> --yarn-orig-ctx <L>` (plus optional YaRN attention-factor / extrapolation-mix overrides), and the equivalent settings travel inside GGUF metadata (`rope.scaling.type = yarn`, `rope.scaling.factor`, `rope.scaling.original_context_length`, …) so a correctly converted model carries its extension recipe with it. **Ollama inherits all of this through its llama-server runner path**: there is no separate Ollama YaRN flag — the scaling a GGUF declares (or that a Modelfile-era override sets) is what the runner applies, and `num_ctx` merely sizes the window the scaled positions must fill (`runners/ollama.md` §4.1, §6.1).
- **The canonical modern use is a shipped model card, not a research demo.** The Qwen3 family is the cleanest documented example: native trained context **32,768**, documented extension to **131,072 via YaRN with factor 4.0 and `original_max_position_embeddings` 32768**, with the llama.cpp invocation above printed on the model cards themselves. Just as important is the warning shipped beside it, paraphrased from the Qwen3 card: static YaRN applies its factor to *every* input regardless of length and can degrade short-text performance, so **if average context does not exceed 32,768, do not enable YaRN** — and if typical length is ~65k, use factor 2.0 rather than 4.0. Qwen's hosted endpoint is documented as using *dynamic* YaRN by default instead. This is the paper's §3.3 static/dynamic distinction surviving, correctly, into production guidance.
- **A necessary correction on Llama 3.1.** Secondary writing (including the LongRoPE2 paper) often says "Llama 3.1 adopts YaRN to reach 128k." Meta's own account describes a different, much heavier road: staged continued pre-training from 8k to 128k over ~800B additional tokens with RoPE base-frequency adjustment, and the shipped HF configuration uses its own `rope_scaling` type `"llama3"` — a frequency-selective scaling in the same intellectual family (leave high frequencies, interpolate low), but not YaRN and not a cheap fine-tune. Treat "Llama 3.1 = YaRN" as shorthand in other papers, not as a verified deployment fact; the verified YaRN deployments at scale are the config-block ecosystems above (Qwen2.5/Qwen3-class models and the long tail of community fine-tunes).
- **Net effect on the field:** YaRN settled the *default* answer to "how do we stretch this RoPE model?" for the 16k–128k era. PI survives as the baseline everyone beats; NTK-aware survives inside YaRN's by-parts rule; and the existence of a standard, config-carried scaling block is what makes the dishonesty in §6 possible — because "128k context" can now be a three-line config change, the claim is cheap to make and must be priced accordingly.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) Ollama on a 6 GB consumer GPU — RoPE scaling trades quality for reach

- **The single most important fact for our fork: scaling changes positional fidelity, not memory.** YaRN makes positions *beyond* the trained length survivable; the KV cache for those positions costs exactly what unscaled positions cost. Per the corpus's KV arithmetic (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §1.2: bytes/token = 2 · layers · KV-heads · head-dim · bytes/element), a Llama-3.1-8B-class GQA model at f16 cache pays **128 KiB/token**: 32k tokens = **4 GiB**, the full YaRN-advertised 128k = **16 GiB** — on a 6 GB card that already holds ~5 GB of Q4 weights. A Llama 2-7B-class MHA model pays **512 KiB/token**: YaRN's own 128k is **64 GiB** of cache. **Longer context is a memory purchase and RoPE scaling does not discount it by one byte.** On 6 GB, the honest menu is: smaller window, KV quantisation (q8_0 ≈ ½, q4_0 ≈ ¼ of f16 cache — `runners/ollama.md` §4.5), a GQA model, or some of each — never "turn on YaRN and set `num_ctx` 131072."
- **Three lengths, always distinguished in the UI.** Every model a runner serves has (i) a *trained* length *L* (GGUF `context_length` / `original_max_position_embeddings`), (ii) a *scaled* length *L′ = s·L* valid only under the scaling configuration the model was fine-tuned or validated with, and (iii) the *allocated* length, the user's `num_ctx`, which is what memory is actually spent on. Ollama today collapses these: it clamps `num_ctx` at the GGUF trained length, silently reduces requests it cannot honour, and reports none of the three in any API response (`runners/ollama.md` §4.1). Our fork should display all three, compute the cache price of the allocated length live from the model's true KV-head count, and label any length above *L* with the scaling method and factor that supposedly legitimises it — "131,072 (YaRN ×4 over a 32,768 trained length, not natively trained)" is an honest claim; "131,072 context" alone is marketing.
- **Applying scaling the model was not extended with is the local-user failure mode this dossier exists to prevent.** Users routinely set `num_ctx` past the trained length, or force `--rope-scaling yarn` on a base model with no YaRN fine-tune, because the flag exists. The paper's evidence bounds what that buys: *without* fine-tuning, YaRN's reliable form is Dynamic-YaRN at **>2×**; the 16×/32× results are *fine-tuned* results, and the LongRoPE paper's non-fine-tuned comparison shows static YaRN applied to a raw LLaMA2-7B actually *collapsing* on PG19 (perplexity 32.64 @ 8k, 87.89 @ 16k — worse than PI; `papers/longrope.md` §4). Guidance the runner should surface, in plain words at the moment of the setting: *beyond ~2× the trained length without a model that was fine-tuned for it, expect quiet degradation — fluent text, worsening recall — not an error.* Quiet is the operative word: perplexity-cliff failure at least announces itself; scaled-but-unfine-tuned failure is a model that keeps chatting while its grip on the early conversation loosens, which in a character chat reads as the character "forgetting" — and will be blamed on the model, not the setting.
- **Static scaling taxes short chats.** Per §5's Qwen guidance and the paper's own short-benchmark table, a static factor chosen for the longest imaginable session degrades every ordinary short session. On a single-user machine the right default is dynamic behaviour (scale follows current length, *s = max(1, l′/L)*) or per-session scaling chosen from the session's actual target length — accepting the §3.3 consequence that changing *s* invalidates post-RoPE cached keys, so scaling must be fixed for the life of a cached prefix. That in turn makes scaling part of runner identity in the Ollama sense (`runners/ollama.md` §3.4): two sessions needing different factors cannot share one resident runner or one prefix cache, and the fork should say so rather than silently reloading.
- **What YaRN does not fix in Ollama's context story:** the two-layer silent truncation (`runners/ollama.md` §4.3) operates *inside* whatever window scaling legitimises. A YaRN-extended 128k window with `num_keep = 4` head preservation is a larger room with the same trapdoor. Scaling policy and eviction policy are separate decisions and must be surfaced separately.

### (b) Project Aesir (Mojo runner — sessions, prefix memory, hybrid SSM models)

- **Implement YaRN as a config-carried RoPE variant, exactly once, in the position-encoding layer** — per-dimension ramp (α, β), temperature folded into the rotary embeddings as the paper prescribes, static and dynamic modes — and treat any deviation (wrong pairing of dimension halves, ramp applied to the wrong axis) as a correctness bug graded by the paper's own test: perplexity at 2× extension on a known Llama-family checkpoint, where a correct implementation degrades gracefully and a wrong one cliffs. RoPE-variant bugs are insidious precisely because output stays fluent at short lengths.
- **Scaling is part of cache identity in Aesir's prefix memory.** Aesir's recorded discipline — prefix snapshots restore bit-exact at identical boundaries (`memory/kv-cache-design.md` §3.4, §4.4) — must key snapshots and shared blocks by (model, quant, *scaling type, factor, original length*) alongside the existing keys. A block computed under YaRN ×4 is a *different tensor* from the same tokens under ×2 or unscaled; reusing it is not an approximation, it is an error of the CacheBlend class without CacheBlend's repair bill.
- **Dynamic scaling vs sessions:** Aesir's resident-session design wants stable prefixes; dynamic YaRN wants *s* to move as the session grows. The reconciliation the paper itself points to (§3.3) is caching keys pre-RoPE and applying rotation at read time — a real kernel-design commitment (rotation becomes part of the attention read path, with its bandwidth cost paid per step) versus the simpler rule "a session pins its scaling at creation; growth past the pin re-bases or restarts the session." Aesir should choose explicitly and record the choice; silently mixing the two is the failure mode.
- **Hybrid SSM models need less of this, on fewer layers.** Only attention layers carry RoPE; SSM layers extrapolate (or fail to) by their own state dynamics. For a hybrid, YaRN applies per attention layer exactly as in a dense model, but the *effective* extension of the whole model is bounded by whichever layer family degrades first — Aesir's long-context grading for hybrids should report attention-layer and SSM-state behaviour separately rather than inheriting a dense model's YaRN verdict.
- **For Aesir's own fine-tunes**, YaRN is the costing baseline for "how cheaply can we extend a house model?": 400 steps / ~256 A100-hours to 64k at 7B scale on PG19-class data, with a second 200-step stage doubling again on the same data. Any Aesir extension plan quoting dramatically less should be asked which of the paper's ingredients (by-parts ramp, temperature, long-data fine-tune) it thinks it can omit — the ablations say each one is carrying weight.

## 7. Cross-links

- **KV-design dossier** (`memory/kv-cache-design.md`) — §1.2/§1.3 for the bytes-per-token arithmetic used in §6 (scaling does not discount the cache); §3.4 for position-binding: a token's cached key encodes its position *under a specific scaling*, which is why scaling changes invalidate caches and why Dynamic-YaRN demands pre-RoPE key storage.
- **Ollama dossier** (`runners/ollama.md`) — §4.1 for `num_ctx` semantics, the VRAM-tier defaults (4,096 on sub-24 GiB hardware) and the silent clamp at GGUF trained length that decides what users actually get when they ask for scaled lengths; §3.4 for the reload trap (load-time options, scaling included, are runner identity); §4.3 for the truncation layers that operate independently of scaling; §4.5 for the KV-quantisation levers that, unlike scaling, *do* change the memory price.
- **StreamingLLM dossier** (`papers/streamingllm.md`) — the other long-chat strategy, and the contrast is fundamental. **Scaling (YaRN/LongRoPE) keeps every token in the window addressable** — attention stays exact over the whole allocated window; what degrades is positional *fidelity* past the trained length, and what grows is the cache bill, linearly and without limit. **Window+anchor (StreamingLLM) keeps positional fidelity perfect inside a bounded window** — every attended token sits at a trained distance — at the price of evicted tokens being *gone*: stability without recall, at constant memory. They answer different questions ("how far can attention reach?" vs "how long can generation run?") and they compose: a YaRN-extended model still needs an eviction policy when the chat outgrows even the scaled window, and StreamingLLM's pinned sinks remain necessary inside it — indeed LongRoPE's searched exemption for initial tokens (`papers/longrope.md` §3) is independent evidence that the head of the sequence is special. A runner that offers only scaling will OOM; a runner that offers only windowing will forget; the honest long-chat product needs both, labelled as what they are.
- **LongRoPE dossier** (`papers/longrope.md`) — the successor method: YaRN's hand-set three-band rule replaced by per-dimension factors found by evolutionary search, plus the progressive fine-tune that reaches 2048k. Read as a pair: YaRN is the analytic answer that shipped everywhere; LongRoPE is the searched answer that shows how much headroom the analytic answer left — and its non-fine-tuned table (§4 there) is the sharpest available warning against applying static YaRN to un-fine-tuned models.
- **GQA dossier** (`papers/gqa.md`) — the other factor in the §6 arithmetic: KV-head count, fixed by the model, decides whether a scaled length is a 16 GiB or a 64 GiB proposition. Scaling decides how far positions *may* reach; GQA decides what reaching costs.
