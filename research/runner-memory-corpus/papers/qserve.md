## 2. The problem as framed

QServe opens with an audit of the field Atom (companion dossier) founded — and the audit is damning. Going beyond INT8, the community's INT4 methods accelerate **low-batch, edge inference** but fail to deliver gains in **large-batch, cloud serving**, the regime they were nominally built for. The paper's exhibit: the state-of-the-art W4A4 serving system, Atom, exhibits **20–25% lower performance than its W4A16 and W8A8 counterparts in TensorRT-LLM** when running Llama-2-7B on A100. The anticipated W4A4 throughput win — mapping computation onto high-throughput 4-bit tensor cores — "has not been consistently observed across current GPU platforms."

The diagnosis is the paper's founding measurement, and it is a *kernel* fact, not an algorithm fact:

- **Dequantisation overhead is 20–90%.** Existing INT4 methods pay significant runtime overhead dequantising either weights or partial sums on GPUs. The reason is an asymmetry inside the GPU itself: tensor cores are extraordinarily fast (A100: 1,248 INT4 / 624 INT8 / 312 FP16 TOPS), while the CUDA cores that must perform dequantisation arithmetic are not — the paper's figure is that **one CUDA-core operation costs as much as ~50 INT4 tensor-core operations** on A100-class hardware. Any scheme whose inner loop interleaves tensor-core GEMM with CUDA-core dequantisation is throttled by the slower unit.
- **Each incumbent precision pays the tax in a different place.** W4A16 must dequantise weights to FP16 inside the GEMM kernel. W4A4, to be accurate at all, needs per-group quantisation of weights *and* activations — and per-group W4A4 forces an integer-to-float dequantisation of **partial sums** (INT4 tensor cores accumulate in INT32) inside the sequential main loop, on CUDA cores. (QuaRot is the paper's example: switching it from per-group to per-channel costs 0.2 perplexity, so the per-group tax is not optional.) W8A8 avoids most of the tax but leaves half the weight bytes and half the tensor-core rate on the table.
- **Roofline consequence.** Plotted on the A100 roofline, W4A16 wins at small batch (memory-bound: fewer weight bytes dominate), W8A8 wins at large batch (compute-bound: INT8 tensor cores dominate), and neither dominates across the serving range. The paper's claim is that a correctly built **W4A8** — 4-bit weight traffic with INT8 tensor-core compute — should dominate *both* across batch sizes, and that KV4 should extend the same logic to attention — **if** the dequantisation tax can be engineered out of the main loop. That conditional is the whole paper.

So where Atom framed the problem as "accuracy at W4A4, without irregular execution," QServe reframes it as: **the format is a kernel-shapes decision.** Quantisation stops being a file format and becomes the whole serving stack — the precision tuple (W4A8KV4) is chosen *because* of what the kernels can execute without CUDA-core stalls, and the kernels are then co-designed around the tuple. A format whose dequantisation the hardware executes reluctantly wins nothing end-to-end, however good its perplexity.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

QServe is the system; **QoQ** (quattuor-octō-quattuor, 4-8-4) is its algorithm: 4-bit weights, 8-bit activations, 4-bit KV cache. All GEMMs run on INT8 tensor cores with FP16 inputs/outputs at block boundaries; attention computes in FP16 on CUDA cores. Three algorithmic pieces and three system pieces, kept distinct because their batch-1 fates (§6) differ.

### The algorithm

**Progressive group quantisation — the centre.** Weights are quantised in two stages. First, per-channel symmetric INT8 quantisation with FP16 scales — but with the INT8 range deliberately shrunk to a **protective range of [−119, 119]** instead of [−127, 127]. Second, those INT8 intermediates are quantised per-group (group size 128 in the g128 configuration) with asymmetric INT4, whose scales and zero-points are themselves stored as UINT8. At runtime the direction reverses inside the GEMM: INT4 weights are dequantised back to INT8 intermediates, and the matmul proceeds exactly as a per-channel W8A8 GEMM on INT8 tensor cores, with the first-level FP16 scale fused into the epilogue. The protective range is the load-bearing detail: without it, the second quantisation step can round an intermediate to a value that dequantises to 128 — outside INT8 range — forcing saturation arithmetic that the paper measures at up to **67% throughput loss**. With the range shrunk by the derived bound (second-level scale ≤ 17, so intermediates must stay ≤ 119.5), overflow is impossible *by construction*, saturation is never invoked, and the INT4→INT8 dequantisation can use full register-level parallelism (below). Contrast with prior two-level schemes (DoubleQuant/VSQuant-style), which quantise the *scales* of a directly-INT4 tensor: their dequantised weights land in FP16, capping throughput at FP16 GEMM rates. QoQ's two levels are ordered so the dequantised tensor lands in **INT8**, which is the entire point.

**SmoothAttention — KV4 made survivable.** Keys carry fixed outlier channels per head (~10× typical magnitudes); values, the paper's profiling shows, do not. At KV8 those outliers are affordable; at KV4 they are not. SmoothAttention applies SmoothQuant's migration *inside the attention score computation*: scale key channel *i* down by λ_i and the corresponding query channel up by the same λ_i — the product **QK**ᵀ is unchanged in exact arithmetic, but the cached keys are smoothed before they are quantised. The decisive simplification versus SmoothQuant: **queries are not quantised**, so there is no second side to balance — no migration-strength search between two quantised tensors; λ_i = max|K_i|^α with α = 0.5 "good enough" in practice. Two engineering details complete it: RoPE pairs channel *i* with channel *i + D/2*, so the scales are constrained λ_i = λ_{i+D/2} to make the scaling commute with RoPE; and with that constraint the scale folds into the projection weights offline (**W**_Q ← Λ**W**_Q, **W**_K ← Λ⁻¹**W**_K), so SmoothAttention costs **zero runtime kernels**. In the ablation it recovers 0.05 perplexity of the 0.14 that KV4 costs, at no system overhead. KV quantisation itself is per-head, asymmetric, INT4, and **dynamic** — FP16 scales/zero-points stored in the cache page beside each head's quantised features and updated on the fly — where vLLM/TensorRT-LLM use per-tensor static KV quantisation; the paper argues per-head dynamic is what KV4 accuracy requires. The cache is paged, following vLLM/TensorRT-LLM, and the system supports in-flight batching.

**General optimisations — the lineage pieces, in the paper's proportions.** (i) **Block-input rotation:** a scaled Hadamard rotation applied to block-input activations (QKV projection, first FFN layer), fused into the previous block's output weights — explicitly inspired by QuaRot (Ashkboos et al. 2024) and QuIP# (Chee et al.); worth −0.18 perplexity in the ablation. (ii) **Block-output smoothing:** SmoothQuant-style diagonal smoothing of block-intermediate activations for output modules (output projection, second FFN layer), with the paper's own correction to SmoothQuant practice — the migration strength should be near 0 here, i.e. the factor is mostly weight-determined, and using input-module strengths *worsens* Llama-2-7B perplexity by up to 0.05. (iii) **Activation-aware channel reordering:** instead of Atom's mixed precision (salient channels held in high precision), channels are reordered so channels of *similar salience* (by max|activation|) fall in the same quantisation group — salience is exploited by grouping, not by exemption; worth −0.03 perplexity. (iv) **Weight clipping** against layer-output (or, for q/k projections, block-output) MSE rather than tensor MSE; worth −0.16 perplexity.

### The system

**Compute-aware weight reordering.** Tensor-core GEMM intrinsics want strided per-thread operand layouts. When storage and compute types match, the `ldmatrix` instruction performs the distribution for free; when they differ (INT4 storage, INT8 compute) it cannot be used, and the fallback is per-thread pointer arithmetic on CUDA cores — the exact scarce resource — plus narrow, bandwidth-wasting loads. QServe's fix is offline: **store the weights in the order the computation consumes them**, tiled so each thread's elements form one consecutive 128-bit word. Pointer arithmetic falls to `ldmatrix` levels, every load is a full 128-bit transaction, and because weights are static the reordering costs nothing at runtime. Scales and zero-points are reordered the same way.

**Fast dequantisation: register-level parallelism + subtraction-after-multiplication.** The INT4→INT8 unpack is arranged (a fixed interleaving of every 32 weights) so three logical operations unpack 32 values via 32-bit register operations. The zero-point subtraction is then *moved out of the main loop algebraically*: rewriting the GEMM shows the subtraction term factorises into an epilogue correction involving the per-token sum of the (unquantised) activations — a quantity precomputable in the preceding memory-bound kernel at negligible cost. For the per-group case, the order matters the other way as well: multiplication-before-subtraction, combined with the protective range's no-overflow guarantee, is what lets both operations use 4-way INT8 register parallelism (`vadd4`-style) instead of scalar steps. This is progressive quantisation and kernel design as a single decision — the algorithm's range bound exists *so that* the kernel's fast path is legal.

**KV4 attention made memory-bound again.** The paper's most instructive systems result: a naive KV4 attention kernel is 1.7× faster than KV8 on L40S but **1.1–1.2× slower on A100** — because fused attention's arithmetic intensity is high enough that INT4 dequantisation (5 ALU ops per element, naively) saturates the A100 FP32 CUDA-core roofline (turning point 9.8 Ops/Byte) and the kernel becomes compute-bound on the wrong cores. QServe pushes from both sides: FP16 instead of FP32 throughout the attention kernel (doubling the compute roof), bit tricks reducing dequantisation to 2 ops/element, simplified control flow and prefetched scales/zero-points. Result: the optimised KV4 kernel beats the TensorRT-LLM KV8 kernel by **1.29–1.51× across sequence lengths 128–1536 on A100** (paper Table 1), and 1.5× in the §5.3 summary — the theoretical KV4 memory saving converted into measured speedup, which is the paper's stated aim.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All figures are the paper's own (v3 / MLSys 2025 version). Hardware: **NVIDIA A100-80GB SXM4 and L40S-48GB**; PyTorch 2.2.0, CUDA 12.2; baselines TensorRT-LLM v0.9.0 (FP16, W8A8, W4A16 configurations), Atom (W4A4) and QuaRot (W4A4) from their main branches as of 18 April 2024. Throughput protocol: maximum achievable throughput under the same device memory budget, input 1024 tokens, output 512 tokens. One configuration nuance the paper states plainly and summaries omit: **per-channel QoQ is used for the A100 throughput results and per-group (g128) for L40S**, because L40S has stronger CUDA cores for dequantisation.

**Throughput — the headline.** Against the *best* TensorRT-LLM configuration per model: Llama-3-8B **1.2× on A100, 1.4× on L40S**; Qwen1.5-72B **2.4× on A100, 3.5× on L40S**. Across the full model set (Llama-3-8B, Llama-2-7B/13B, Mistral-7B, Llama-1-30B, Yi-34B, Llama-2-70B, Qwen1.5-72B), speedups over best-TensorRT-LLM run 1.17–2.38× on A100 and 1.39–3.47× on L40S (paper Table 4, absolute tokens/s given there — e.g. Llama-2-7B: 2,908 tok/s QServe vs 2,334 best-baseline on A100; 2,394 vs 1,271 on L40S). The paper's geometric-mean summary: **2.36× over TensorRT-LLM on L40S, 1.68× on A100**. Against the W4A4 systems directly, on A100: **2.5–2.9× over Atom and QuaRot** (introduction) — the systems that, per §2, did not themselves beat TensorRT-LLM. The cost claim follows from the L40S results: QServe on the cheaper L40S matches or beats TensorRT-LLM on A100 for most models at the same batch size (the memory saved by W4+KV4 is what lets the 48 GB card hold A100-sized batches), stated as a **3× reduction in dollar cost** of serving. Same-batch decomposition (Llama-2-7B, L40S): the 1.88× total splits into 1.45× same-batch kernel speedup × 1.3× from the enlarged batch the memory savings admit — like Atom's headline, the number compounds a kernel win with a capacity win, and the paper, to its credit, publishes the split.

**Kernel-level.** QServe's per-group W4A8 GEMM: **1.5× over W8A8 cuBLAS GEMM** (§4.1). Dequantisation overhead: comparable to TensorRT-LLM W4A16's, while computing on INT8 tensor cores at 2× W4A16's arithmetic rate (Figure 18 discussion) — against up to 90% dequantisation overhead measured for Atom-W4A4. KV4 attention: §3 above (naive KV4 *loses* on A100; optimised KV4 wins 1.29–1.51× over KV8).

**Accuracy — perplexity.** WikiText-2, sequence 2048 (paper Table 2). QoQ W4A8KV4 g128 vs FP16: Llama-3-8B 6.70 vs 6.14 (+0.56); Llama-2-7B 5.67 vs 5.47 (+0.20); Llama-2-13B 5.06 vs 4.88 (+0.18); Llama-2-70B 3.46 vs 3.32 (+0.14); Mistral-7B 5.41 vs 5.25; Mixtral-8x7B 4.13 vs 3.84; Yi-34B 4.73 vs 4.60. The paper's summary: versus W8A8 SmoothQuant and W4A16 AWQ, QoQ's perplexity increase is at most ~0.16 worse on Llama-2-7B-class comparisons while running a strictly lower-bit format; QoQ beats Atom at both W4A4 and W4A8KV4 settings and improves on W4A4 QuaRot by up to 0.49. Note the honest ordering in the table: QoQ does **not** beat W4A16 AWQ/GPTQ-R on perplexity (Llama-2-7B: AWQ 5.60, QoQ-g128 5.67) — its claim is throughput at near-W4A16 accuracy, not accuracy leadership. Calibration note, following corpus discipline: QoQ's own calibration set is not named in the evaluation section as read; the paper specifies calibration datasets (Pile validation; WikiText-2 in the greyed rows) for the **Atom and QuaRot baselines it re-evaluated**, and flags WikiText-2-calibrated results in grey in its own table — readers should treat QoQ's WikiText-2 perplexity with the standard caution until its calibration provenance is pinned from the released code.

**Accuracy — zero-shot and long-context.** Five tasks (PIQA, ARC-e/c, HellaSwag, WinoGrande), Llama-2 (paper Table 3): QoQ's stated average losses versus FP16 are **1.03 points at 7B, 0.89 at 13B, 0.40 at 70B** (g128 averages from the table: 67.95 vs 68.98; 70.83 vs 71.72; 76.40 vs 76.57 — the 70B table averages imply a smaller gap than the text's 0.40, a minor internal inconsistency flagged here rather than resolved). QoQ beats QuaRot on WinoGrande by up to 4.82 points, per the text. LongBench on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct (W4A8KV4 g128): average 38.38 vs BF16's 38.52 across ten tasks — the KV4 long-context claim, and the result most relevant to §6.

**Ablation — the cost/benefit ledger** (Llama-2-7B, paper Figure 16 discussion). W8A8 RTN baseline → weights to 4 bits: perplexity worsens significantly, speed +1.12×, memory −3.5 GB → block-input rotation: −0.18 perplexity → weight clipping: −0.16 → KV cache to 4 bits: +0.14 perplexity, but **+1.47× end-to-end throughput and GPU memory halved** → SmoothAttention: −0.05, zero system overhead → progressive group quantisation: −0.04, negligible overhead → channel reordering: −0.03. Every algorithmic piece is worth hundredths of a perplexity point; the *system* pieces are worth the multiples. That ratio is the paper in one paragraph.

## 5. Position in the lineage

The chain, with each step's assumption about its predecessor stated as this wave's brief requires:

**SmoothQuant (migration) →** proved a calibration-fitted diagonal transform can move quantisation difficulty from activations to weights and make W8A8 lossless. *Assumed by the next step:* that the outlier problem is a *distribution* problem solvable tensor-by-tensor — and that 8 bits of headroom exists on the receiving side.

**Atom (systematised mixed precision) →** assumed SmoothQuant's diagnosis but not its remedy: at W4A4, migration has nowhere to go, so Atom segregates outliers into INT8, group-quantises the rest, and fuses everything. *Assumed by the next step:* that accuracy at the target precision is the binding constraint, and that a sufficiently careful algorithm makes the low-bit GEMM fast by construction. QServe's founding measurement is precisely the falsification of that second assumption — Atom's system, transplanted into TensorRT-LLM's comparison, *loses* to higher-precision configurations.

**QuaRot (rotation theory) →** assumed both predecessors were fighting outliers in the wrong basis: rotate, and the outliers dissolve, no exemptions needed. It solved the *accuracy* of end-to-end W4A4KV4 more cleanly than Atom (zero outlier channels). *Assumed by the next step:* that an outlier-free distribution plus INT4 tensor cores equals speed. QServe's KV4 analysis names the flaw directly — QuaRot's online Hadamard transforms add compute *inside the attention operator*, on CUDA cores, making real speedup over KV8 hard to achieve; and its per-group W4A4 GEMM pays the partial-sum dequantisation tax. Rotation solved the statistics and ignored the main loop.

**QServe (rotation + system co-design) →** assumes accuracy is *solved enough* by the accumulated toolkit — and indeed QoQ is deliberately eclectic: QuaRot's rotation for block inputs, SmoothQuant's smoothing for block outputs, Atom's reordering recast as salience grouping, AWQ's activation-aware salience, GPTQ-lineage clipping — and spends its originality on the two things none of the predecessors centred: a precision tuple chosen for kernel executability (W4A8, retreating from W4A4's tensor cores because INT8 tensor cores plus cheap dequantisation beat INT4 tensor cores plus expensive dequantisation), and kernels co-designed so the CUDA cores stop being the bottleneck (protective range → register parallelism; reordered storage → no pointer arithmetic; subtraction deferred to the epilogue; attention pushed back into the memory-bound region). The lineage's lesson inverts across the chain: SmoothQuant–QuaRot progress by making quantisation *error* smaller; QServe progresses by making quantisation *overhead* smaller, accepting slightly worse error than W4A16 in exchange.

**The honest counterpoint (wave 16's verdict, carried explicitly).** These wins are measured at serving batch sizes on datacenter GPUs — maximum-throughput batching on A100/L40S, 1024-in/512-out workloads, throughput per dollar as the objective. The batch-1 personal case inverts several premises: there is no batch for the capacity win to enlarge (half of QServe's Llama-2-7B headline is the enlarged-batch factor); decode GEMMs are GEMVs whose intensity is ~1 MAC/element, so the INT8 tensor-core rate that justifies W4A8 over W4A16 is unreachable; and the CUDA-core dequantisation tax, while real at batch 1, is paid on *weight* streaming that a weight-only runner already fuses into its mat-vec kernels as its core design. What does *not* invert: attention's KV traffic is per-token, per-step memory movement at any batch size, and the memory-bound character QServe engineers its KV4 kernel around is the *same* character batch-1 attention has. §6 grades the pieces individually; the tension is stated, not waved away — QServe is simultaneously a datacenter artifact and the source of the one idea the local runner most needs.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) A 6 GB batch-1 runner — which pieces survive

Roofline terms, as in the companion dossier: at batch 1, decode dense layers are GEMVs at ~1 MAC per loaded element; token rate is weight-bytes ÷ bandwidth; tensor-core peak rates at any precision are unreachable and therefore irrelevant to format choice. Against that:

- **KV4 with SmoothAttention — SURVIVES, and is the strongest single transfer in either dossier of this wave.** Three reasons, in order of weight. (i) *The traffic argument is batch-independent:* KV cache is streamed in full on every decode step at batch 1 exactly as at batch 64 — it is the one tensor whose movement quantisation reduces without needing arithmetic intensity to rise. On the 6 GB target it is also the capacity argument: the corpus's worked case (`memory/kv-cache-design.md`, IDEA-LEDGER #3/#19) shows KV precision deciding whether an 8B model's 8K context fits beside its weights at all; KV4 roughly doubles q8_0's token budget again. (ii) *SmoothAttention itself is free at runtime* — a diagonal rescaling folded into **W**_Q/**W**_K offline, with the RoPE-commutation constraint solved in the paper. A local converter can apply it at quantisation time to any model whose KV will be cached at 4 bits; no kernel support is needed for the smoothing, only for the KV4 cache itself. (iii) *The accuracy evidence is the right shape:* the LongBench near-parity (38.38 vs 38.52) is long-context evidence, not just WikiText-2 — the regime where KV4 would be used locally is the regime it was validated in. Caveats carried honestly: the local ecosystem's incumbent KV4 form (GGUF q4_0-class, and the corpus's #19 asymmetric design — K per-channel, V per-token) was designed without SmoothAttention, and K is empirically the more sensitive cache; SmoothAttention is a *converter-side improvement to* that design, to be graded per model family under #34 before becoming a default — labelled judgment, not a paper result. **This is the piece of QServe that survives the batch-1 collapse.**
- **Progressive quantisation's two-level scales — SURVIVES as a weight format, already converged.** QoQ's structure — a coarse per-channel FP16 scale over per-group UINT8 scales over INT4 values — is, in shape, the GGUF K-quant structure (fp16 super-block scale over quantised sub-block scales over 4-bit values; `runners/llamacpp-ggml.md`). Two ecosystems again converged on hierarchical scales for grouped 4-bit weights. What does *not* transfer is progressive quantisation's *purpose*: its levels are ordered so runtime dequantisation lands in INT8 for a tensor-core GEMM. A batch-1 runner dequantises into registers for a mat-vec and has no INT8 intermediate to land in — it should keep the hierarchy (it is already keeping it) and ignore the ordering rationale.
- **Compute-aware weight reordering — SURVIVES as a principle, invisibly.** "Store weights in the order the kernel consumes them; layout is offline and free" is already the GGUF world's practice (per-type block layouts designed around their SIMD/mat-vec kernels). QServe's contribution to a local runner is confirmatory and diagnostic: when a local kernel is slow, layout-versus-consumption mismatch is a named suspect, priced in the paper at the difference between `ldmatrix`-class access and pointer-arithmetic access.
- **W4A8 activation quantisation, the GEMM machinery, register-level dequantisation — MOOT at batch 1, for the roofline reason.** Quantising a single-row activation saves no meaningful traffic; there is no GEMM main loop to protect; the INT8 tensor cores cannot be fed by a GEMV. The subtraction-after-multiplication algebra and the protective range are solutions to a problem (main-loop CUDA-core stalls in a batched INT4/INT8 GEMM) that the local runner does not have. They become relevant only if the local runner grows a *batched prefill* path that is compute-bound — long-prompt ingestion on the 6 GB card is the one local phase where prefill intensity rises, and even there the batch is tokens-of-one-request, the weights are already resident, and the incumbent answer is chunked prefill, not a second GEMM precision path. Labelled judgment: do not import.
- **The KV4-attention kernel lesson — SURVIVES as a warning.** QServe's naive-KV4 slowdown on A100 is the general law in miniature: *a lower-bit cache whose dequantisation costs more arithmetic than the attention it feeds will lose to a higher-bit cache, on any hardware where the dequantiser is the slow unit.* On the local card the slow unit is the CPU/GPU scalar path beside the mat-vec; any local KV4 implementation must be graded on measured decode tok/s at long context, not on bytes saved — bytes are the promise, the kernel is the delivery.

### (b) The build campaign's quant provisioning (IDEA-LEDGER #42 lineage)

Labelled judgment, extending #42's provision-by-lineage rule with this wave's evidence:

- **Default format per device class — datacenter/serving class:** provision **W4A8KV4 (QoQ lineage)** as the default low-bit serving format in preference to W4A4 (Atom/QuaRot lineage), on QServe's evidence: at A100/L40S scale it is the only sub-8-bit format family shown beating best-configuration TensorRT-LLM end-to-end, and its accuracy sits within ~0.2 perplexity of W4A16 AWQ on the Llama-2 line. W4A4 provisions only where a specific system's own end-to-end measurements (not its paper's kernel measurements) justify it. The provisioning unit, as with Atom, is (format, kernel library, GPU generation) — QServe's own per-channel-on-A100 / per-group-on-L40S split proves the same format is a different good on different silicon.
- **Default format per device class — personal/batch-1 class (the 6 GB target):** weights from the **weight-only grouped-4-bit lineage** (GGUF K-quant family, per #42's existing rule); KV provisioned **separately and asymmetrically** — q8_0 default per #3, with a **KV4 tier (SmoothAttention-smoothed at conversion time) as the labelled long-context option** per #19/#20, gated on per-family #34 grading. QServe changes the KV4 tier's expected quality, not its risk class: it remains an experiment-tier provision until the local grading exists, because QServe's KV4 evidence is A100/L40S CUDA-kernel evidence and the local KV4 kernel is a different implementation of the same idea.
- **What QServe implies for provisioning *process*:** the paper's deepest provisioning lesson is that **bit-width tuples are not comparable across lineages** — W4A4 (Atom) and W4A8KV4 (QoQ) and W4A16 (AWQ) are three different trades of accuracy against *kernel executability*, and the winner was decided by dequantisation overhead, a property no file header records. #42's inventory should therefore carry, for every serving-family artifact, its measured dequantisation/kernel overhead class alongside its lineage — or, where unmeasured, the explicit label "kernel behaviour unprovenanced," graded like an uncalibrated quantiser.

- **Roofline framing:** `memory/memory-wall-and-roofline.md` (QServe §3 is a worked roofline argument — W4A16/W8A8/W4A8 dominance regions and the CUDA-core turning point that decides KV4 — and belongs in that dossier's case set).
- **Production stacks:** `runners/tensorrt-llm.md` (the baseline system in every QServe throughput claim — its FP16/W8A8/W4A16 configurations define the bar, and its KV8 attention kernel is the substrate QServe's KV4 kernel modifies), `runners/vllm.md` (paged KV and in-flight batching — QServe adopts both, changing only the KV precision and its per-head dynamic quantisation; where QoQ-class algorithms landed in production stacks is documented across these two dossiers).
