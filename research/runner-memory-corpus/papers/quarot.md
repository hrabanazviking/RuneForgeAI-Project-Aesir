## 2. The problem as framed

Wave 5's canon (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, QuIP# — see §7) left a specific residue, and QuaRot is framed directly against it. Weight-only quantisation was solved to a useful standard at 4 bits, but *end-to-end* low-bit inference — weights, activations, **and** KV cache all in the low-bit GEMM — still broke, for one reason: **activation outliers**. A small set of hidden-state channels carries values orders of magnitude larger than the rest, persistently, across tokens. Any per-tensor or per-token activation scale is set by those channels, so the bulk of the distribution is crushed into a handful of INT4 levels.

The prior answers each left the hardest tensors unquantised or paid for them in mixed precision:

- **SmoothQuant-style migration** (diagonal per-channel rescaling fitted on calibration data) moves difficulty from activations onto weights and works at W8A8, but at 4 bits the migrated problem reappears on the weight side and the method collapses — in QuaRot's own Table 1, 4-bit SmoothQuant scores WikiText-2 perplexity 83.12 on Llama-2-7B against an FP16 baseline of 5.47.
- **Outlier-keeping methods** (LLM.int8()-style decomposition, QUIK, Atom) identify the offending channels and hold them in higher precision. They work, but they need outlier-aware mixed-precision kernels, reordering machinery, and they characteristically exempt exactly the tensors that matter most — QUIK, for instance, keeps the down-projection in 8 bits in its standard configuration.
- **The down-projection input and the KV cache are the worst cases.** The input to `W_down` is the product of a gated activation and is notoriously outlier-heavy; keys carry their own channel outliers, so a naively quantised KV cache destroys attention long before weights do.

QuaRot's framing question is therefore not "how do we round better?" but "can the outlier channels be made to *not exist* — for every tensor in the network, with no channel exempted and no calibration-fitted special cases?" Its answer is to change the basis: rotate the hidden state by an orthogonal transform so outlier energy is spread over all coordinates before anything is quantised.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

**The invariance QuaRot exploits.** A transformer with pre-norm (RMSNorm) blocks has a *computational invariance*, imported from the same authors' SliceGPT work: insert an orthogonal matrix **Q** on the residual stream and its transpose **Q**ᵀ into the adjacent weight matrices, and the full-precision function is exactly unchanged. This works because RMSNorm divides each row by its norm, and an orthogonal transform preserves norms — formally, RMSNorm(**X**) = RMSNorm(**XQ**ᵀ)**Q** once the RMSNorm's learned per-channel scale diag(**α**) has first been absorbed into the neighbouring weight matrices. The rotated network computes the same outputs; only the *coordinates* in which activations are expressed have changed.

**Why rotation kills outliers.** QuaRot uses a **randomised Hadamard matrix** as **Q**: a Walsh–Hadamard matrix (entries ±1/√d, built recursively as **H**₂ₙ = **H**₂ ⊗ **H**₂ₙ₋₁) composed with a random ±1 sign diagonal, following QuIP#'s incoherence-processing precedent. A Hadamard transform mixes every input coordinate into every output coordinate with equal weight. A single huge channel therefore contributes a small amount to *all* rotated coordinates instead of dominating one: the rotated distribution's max-to-typical ratio collapses (the paper's Figure 1 shows the FFN-input distribution of Llama-2-7B layer 10 going from spiked to near-Gaussian), and both weights and activations become *incoherent* in QuIP's sense — max(**W**) ≤ μ‖**W**‖_F/√(mn) with small μ — which is precisely the condition under which plain scalar quantisation has bounded, well-behaved error. Dimensions that are not powers of two are handled by factoring d = 2ⁿ·m and using a Kronecker product with a known Hadamard matrix of size m, at O(d(m+n)) cost; the pure power-of-two case is the Fast Walsh–Hadamard Transform at O(d log₂ d), with no floating-point multiplies (entries are ±1).

**Where the rotations sit — four placements, three absorbed, two genuinely online.**

1. **Residual-stream rotation (absorbed).** One global randomised Hadamard **Q** is applied to the hidden state between blocks. Input-side matrices are pre-multiplied by **Q**ᵀ·diag(**α**) (e.g. **W**_k ← **Q**ᵀ diag(**α**) **W**_k) and output-side matrices post-multiplied by **Q**, so no runtime operation remains — the rotation lives inside the stored weights. The activations passed between blocks are now the rotated, outlier-free ones, and they are quantised per-token, symmetrically, on the fly.
2. **FFN down-projection (online Hadamard — the place absorption fails).** Inside the FFN, the input to **W**_down is produced by a non-linearity (SiLU-gated product), so no weight on its left exists to absorb a rotation into. QuaRot therefore inserts an explicit **online Hadamard transform** on that activation in FP16 at runtime, and pre-multiplies **W**_down by **H** (making it **HW**_down**Q** overall) so the transform is undone algebraically by the matmul itself. This is the one structural addition to the forward pass in the MLP, and it exists precisely because the hardest tensor in the network cannot be reached by absorption.
3. **Value path (absorbed, head-wise).** Within each head, **W**_v and **W**_out are implicitly multiplied together through the attention weights, so a head-dimension Hadamard can be fused into both: **W**_v⁽ʰ⁾ ← **W**_v⁽ʰ⁾**H**, **W**_out⁽ʰ⁾ ← **H W**_out⁽ʰ⁾. The values that enter the KV cache are thereby rotated and outlier-free. A Kronecker identity lets most of the cross-head part be fused into **W**_out as well, leaving one small online "Hadamard heads" block on the attention output.
4. **Key path (online Hadamard, post-RoPE).** Keys cannot be rotated by absorption because RoPE sits between **W**_q/**W**_k and the attention scores and does not commute with a fused rotation. QuaRot applies an online head-wise Hadamard to queries and keys *after* RoPE; since both sides are rotated by the same orthogonal matrix, the scores **QK**ᵀ are unchanged, while the cached keys are the rotated, quantisable versions. The paper explicitly chooses post-RoPE caching over pre-RoPE caching so that at decode time only the single new token's key is transformed, not the whole cache per query.

**Quantisation on top.** With the model rotated, Stage 2 is deliberately plain: weights are quantised with GPTQ (per-column symmetric; round-to-nearest also evaluated), activations per-token symmetric with clipping ratio 0.9, KV cache asymmetric with group size 128 and clipping 0.95, all matmuls in INT4 with INT32 accumulation. The paper counts 1½ Hadamard transforms per layer at runtime (versus two per weight matrix for QuIP-style processing that undoes its transforms in the forward pass).

**What "outlier-free" means, precisely, in their terms.** It is a claim about the *rotated* tensors, not about information being deleted: after rotation, no coordinate of the hidden states, FFN intermediates, or KV entries carries the outsized magnitudes present in the original basis (their Figure 1 evidence), so a single per-token scale quantises every channel with comparable relative error and **zero channels need to be identified and retained in higher precision** — the "#Outlier Features: 0" column in their Table 1, against QUIK's 256 and Atom's 128. "Outlier-free" does not mean error-free: §4's perplexity losses are the price that remains.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All figures are the paper's own, from the v2 (NeurIPS) version, on the **Llama-2 family**, unless stated. Wave-5 grading caution applies throughout: perplexity and downstream accuracy are different instruments and are kept separate below, and the calibration/eval relationship is stated explicitly.

**Perplexity — WikiText-2, W4A4KV4 end-to-end, GPTQ weights (paper Table 1).** FP16 baselines: 7B 5.47, 13B 4.88, 70B 3.32.

| Method | Outlier channels kept | 7B | 13B | 70B |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SmoothQuant (RTN) | 0 | 83.12 | 35.88 | — |
| OmniQuant (RTN) | 0 | 14.26 | 12.30 | — |
| QUIK-4B (GPTQ) | 256 | 8.87 | 7.78 | 6.91 |
| **QuaRot (GPTQ)** | **0** | **6.10** | **5.40** | **3.79** |
| Atom-128G (GPTQ, group 128) | 128 | 6.03 | 5.26 | — |
| QuaRot-128G (group 128) | 0 | 5.93 | 5.26 | 3.61 |

So the headline "at most 0.47 perplexity loss" is the **70B** figure (3.79 − 3.32); the 7B loss is 0.63 and the 13B loss 0.52. Group-wise QuaRot matches Atom's perplexity while keeping no outlier channels in high precision. With round-to-nearest weights instead of GPTQ (paper Table 3), 4-bit perplexity degrades to 8.37 (7B) / 4.14 (70B) — the rotation alone does not make RTN sufficient at small scale; GPTQ is load-bearing in the headline numbers. At 6 and 8 bits the paper reports RTN quantisation as **lossless** on Llama-2 (8-bit RTN: 5.50 vs 5.47 on 7B; 3.33 vs 3.32 on 70B), with no calibration data at all.

**Downstream — zero-shot accuracy (paper Table 2).** Six tasks via LM Evaluation Harness (PIQA, WinoGrande, HellaSwag, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, LAMBADA), W4A4KV4 with GPTQ. Average accuracy, FP16 → QuaRot: 7B 69.82 → 65.64 (−4.18 points, the worst case); 13B 72.59 → 69.79; 70B 77.07 → 75.98 (−1.09 points). The abstract's "retains 99% of the zero-shot performance" is therefore a **70B** statement (≈98.6%); at 7B the retention is ≈94%. Read the claim as scale-dependent, as the paper's own table shows it to be.

**Speed and memory — as reported, on an NVIDIA RTX 3090, measured on a single transformer block** (the full model at large batch did not fit their cluster — the paper says so, and the whole-model figures are extrapolations). Prefill (sequence length 2048, compute-bound): 1.97–2.16× over FP16 on Llama-2-7B, rising with batch size; up to 3.33× on the 70B block at batch 64. Decode memory (batch 16, 50 generated tokens): at least 3.63× peak-memory saving versus FP16, up to 3.75× (7B) and 3.89× (70B). Note the paper's conclusion section quotes the older 2.16× / 3.39× pairing — the §5.2 numbers above are the v2 body's own and differ; treat any single quoted QuaRot speedup with suspicion until its section and version are named. Kernels: CUTLASS INT4 TensorCore GEMM, FlashInfer for the quantised KV cache; these are research kernels, not a production serving stack.

**Cost and calibration — the separation audit.** Applying the rotation to Llama-2-70B takes ~5 minutes on one A100; GPTQ quantisation a further ~2 hours. Calibration: GPTQ uses **128 samples from the WikiText-2 *training* set** at sequence length 2048, and perplexity is reported on WikiText-2 (test) — train/test separation is maintained for the quantiser, in line with wave-5 discipline. The rotation itself is **calibration-free** (randomised Hadamard, fixed by seed, not fitted), which is QuaRot's cleanest methodological property: nothing in the transform has seen any data. The zero-shot tasks are entirely disjoint from the calibration corpus. What the paper does *not* provide: any evaluation on a perplexity corpus other than WikiText-2, so cross-corpus generalisation of the 0.47–0.63 losses is asserted by the downstream tasks only, not by a second language-modelling benchmark.

## 5. Position in the lineage

**Versus QuIP# (wave 5).** QuIP# already used randomised-Hadamard *incoherence processing* — but for **weights only**, in a weight-only quantiser: it conjugates each weight matrix (and its Hessian) by orthogonal transforms, quantises the rotated weights against an E8 lattice codebook, and then *undoes* the transforms on the activations at inference, paying two transforms per matrix in the forward pass. QuaRot's own related-work section states the delta precisely, and it is threefold: (i) instead of undoing the transform, QuaRot uses SliceGPT's computational invariance to **fuse the rotation into the weights permanently**, so the *activations themselves live in the rotated basis* between blocks; (ii) the rotation is thereby extended **through the whole network** — residual stream, FFN intermediates (via the online Hadamard at the down-projection), values, and keys/KV cache — making W4A4KV4 end-to-end possible, which no QuIP-line method attempts; (iii) the quantiser on top reverts to plain scalar GPTQ/RTN — no lattice codebook, no vector quantisation — because once activations are incoherent too, scalar 4-bit suffices. In short: QuIP# rotates to make *weights* quantisable at 2 bits; QuaRot rotates to make *everything* quantisable at 4 bits.

**Versus SmoothQuant (wave 5).** Migration and rotation are the two structural answers to the same outlier. SmoothQuant applies a *diagonal* transform: per-channel scales s_j, fitted from calibration activations, shrink outlier activation channels and inflate the corresponding weight rows by the inverse — difficulty is *moved*, not removed, the transform is data-fitted, and at 4 bits the inflated weights become the new problem (Table 1's collapse). Rotation applies a *dense orthogonal* transform: no channel is singled out, outlier energy is *dissolved* across all coordinates, the transform is data-independent, and the price is paid in runtime transforms where absorption fails rather than in weight dynamic range. AWQ (wave 5) sits between them — diagonal like SmoothQuant, but activation-aware-searched and weight-only in scope. The lineage lesson: diagonal transforms redistribute a fixed amount of difficulty between two tensors; orthogonal transforms reduce the total difficulty by changing what a "channel" is.

**What QuaRot leaves open** — and the next dossier takes up: the rotation is *random*. Nothing in QuaRot chooses **Q** to suit this model's actual distributions; any randomised Hadamard is expected to work by the incoherence argument. SpinQuant's founding observation is that this expectation hides enormous variance, and that the rotation is therefore a free parameter waiting to be optimised.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) The GGUF world — labelled judgment

Our quantisation is weight-only, offline, and format-defined (Q4_K and kin): the runner dequantises weights and computes in FP16/FP32; activations and (by default) the KV cache are not INT4, and there is no INT4 GEMM path for a rotation to feed. Judged against that world:

- **The absorbed part is, in principle, carryable.** A residual-stream rotation fused into the weights is just a different set of full-precision weights. A converter could rotate a model offline, quantise the rotated weights with the existing Q4_K machinery, and ship a GGUF that a completely unmodified runner executes — the runner cannot tell the difference, because computationally there is none. Whether rotated weights quantise *better* under Q4_K's grouped scalar scheme is an empirical question the paper does not answer (its weight quantiser is per-column GPTQ, not K-quants), but the incoherence argument transfers in kind: rotated weights have flatter per-group ranges, and flat ranges are what grouped scalar quantisation wants. This is the one QuaRot idea a weight-only world can adopt for free, and its expected gain is *modest* — at 4 bits weight-only, GPTQ/AWQ-class GGUF artifacts are already close to FP16; rotation would be competing for the last fraction of a perplexity point, not rescuing a collapse.
- **The online Hadamard is the part that does not carry.** The down-projection transform, the head-wise value transform's online remainder, and the post-RoPE key transform are *runtime operations on activations*. A ggml-class runner could implement a fast Walsh–Hadamard kernel — it is O(d log d) of additions, cheaper than the matmuls around it — but in a weight-only runner it would buy nothing: it exists to make *activation and KV* quantisation accurate, and if activations stay in FP16 there is no activation-quantisation error for it to reduce. Adopting it only makes sense as part of adopting W4A4KV4 inference wholesale — new INT4 GEMM kernels, quantised-KV attention, per-token activation quantisation — which is a different runner, not a GGUF feature.
- **Does the gain matter at batch-1 decode? Largely no — labelled judgment.** QuaRot's wins are prefill speed (compute-bound, large batches of tokens) and KV/activation memory at long context and large batch. Single-user local decode is weight-bandwidth-bound: the tokens/second are set by how fast 4-bit weights stream from memory, the activation is a *single row*, and per-token activation quantisation error on one row is a second-order effect next to weight error. The KV-cache term is the only one that grows into relevance for a local user, and only at long contexts — and the GGUF world already addresses KV memory with its own (optional, much simpler) KV quantisation, accepting per-channel key handling rather than rotation. Verdict: **for a weight-only GGUF runner at batch-1, QuaRot is a lineage landmark and a possible offline weight pre-pass experiment — not a missing feature.** Its natural home is the batched-serving world (vLLM-class) that wave 5's serving dossiers describe.

### (b) Aesir's compression school — a question, framed with its grading discipline

Aesir's school writes its own packers (Q8_0/Q4_K-class) and grades packed-versus-original. *Question:* is a randomised-Hadamard rotation pre-pass — rotate the FP weights offline, then pack with the school's existing scalar packer, no other change — a legitimate school experiment? Framed as a question, with the discipline it would need:

- **It is cheap and falsifiable**, which is what makes it school-worthy: the transform is ~50 lines of well-specified mathematics, needs no calibration data (QuaRot's rotation is data-independent — a methodological virtue under the wave-5 rule "grade on data the quantiser never saw", since here the transform has seen *no* data at all), and its prediction is sharp: rotated-then-packed weights should show lower packed-vs-original error than packed-only weights at the same bit-width, with the advantage *growing* as bits drop (QuaRot's RTN ablation and QuIP#'s ablations both predict near-nothing at 8 bits, something at 4, most at 2–3).
- **Grading discipline it would need, non-negotiable:** (i) the corpus's raw-oracle protocol — teacher-forced rank and logit-gap of packed-rotated vs the *original unrotated* model on identical raw prompts, never against a template-rendered comparator; (ii) held-out evaluation text disjoint from anything used anywhere in the pipeline (trivially satisfiable here, but it must be *shown*, not assumed); (iii) the control must be the same packer on unrotated weights at the same bits and same group sizes — the experiment is rotation-on vs rotation-off, nothing else moving; (iv) report per-layer max-to-RMS (incoherence) before and after rotation alongside the grade, so a null result is *explained* (rotation failed to flatten, or flattening failed to help this packer) rather than merely recorded.
- **What it must not claim:** any result about activations or KV — a weight-only packer experiment tests exactly one of QuaRot's four placements, and a positive result would license "rotation helps our weight packing", not "QuaRot works". The school's standing rule applies: grade the artifact you built, not the paper you read.
