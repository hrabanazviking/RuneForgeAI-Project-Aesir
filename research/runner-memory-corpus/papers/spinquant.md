## 2. The problem as framed

SpinQuant starts where QuaRot (this wave's companion dossier) stops. QuaRot established that a *randomised* Hadamard rotation, fused into a transformer's weights via computational invariance, dissolves activation outliers and makes end-to-end W4A4KV4 quantisation possible. But QuaRot treats the rotation as a generic device: any random rotation is expected to work, because the incoherence argument is statistical.

SpinQuant's founding measurement is that this expectation is false in the way that matters. The authors quantised LLaMA-2-7B to W4A4 under **100 different random rotations** and measured downstream zero-shot accuracy for each: the spread between the best and worst rotation is **up to 13 accuracy points**. Random Hadamard rotations do better than random dense orthogonal rotations (consistent with QuIP#'s tighter incoherence bounds for Hadamard matrices) but still spread by up to **6 points**. In other words, the rotation is not a neutral basis change — it is a high-variance hyperparameter sitting in front of every quantisation decision in the network, and the field was drawing it at random.

The problem as framed is therefore an optimisation problem that QuaRot's framing made visible but did not pose: *the set of rotations that leave the full-precision network exactly unchanged is a large manifold; the quantised network's loss varies enormously over that manifold; find the point on the manifold that minimises the quantised loss.* The residue from wave 5 is inherited unchanged — activation outliers, the down-projection input, and the KV cache are still the tensors that break end-to-end 4-bit — but the proposed remedy is no longer a better fixed transform. It is to *learn* the transform, per model, on calibration data, while the weights themselves stay frozen.

## 3. Core mechanism in our own words

**The parameterisation — four rotations, two learned, two kept Hadamard.** SpinQuant first catalogues every place a rotation can be inserted into a (pre-norm, LLaMA-style) transformer while preserving exact full-precision equivalence, re-deriving the same invariance QuaRot uses (RMSNorm scales are first absorbed into adjacent weights):

- **R1 — residual-stream rotation** (shape D_token × D_token, one per model, shared across layers): rotates the hidden state between blocks; absorbed into the embedding, all input-side projections (Q/K/V/up/gate), all output-side projections, and the LM head. This is QuaRot's global **Q**, made learnable.
- **R2 — value/output rotation** (shape D_head × D_head, learned **separately per layer**): rotates the value vectors within each head and is undone by R2ᵀ at the out-projection input; absorbed into **W**_v and **W**_o. This placement has no QuaRot counterpart in learned form — QuaRot fuses a fixed head-wise Hadamard here; SpinQuant makes the per-layer matrix itself a free orthogonal parameter, which is what lets it attack *in-block* outliers rather than only between-block ones.
- **R3 — query/key rotation** (online Hadamard, post-RoPE, for the KV cache) and **R4 — FFN down-projection rotation** (online Hadamard on the gated activation before **W**_down): exactly the two places where absorption is impossible (RoPE in the way; a non-linearity in the way — see the QuaRot dossier §3). SpinQuant **keeps these fixed as Hadamard matrices**, explicitly because the fast Walsh–Hadamard transform makes an online Hadamard nearly free, whereas a learned dense online rotation would need a full matmul at runtime. The learning is spent only where it costs nothing at inference.

This yields the paper's two configurations: **SpinQuant_no_had** = learned R1 + R2 only, fully absorbed — inference is the *original architecture with different weights*, no new kernels, no forward-pass change; **SpinQuant_had** = R1 + R2 learned plus R3 + R4 online Hadamards, for the extreme W4A4KV4 regime.

**The optimisation — Cayley SGD on the Stiefel manifold.** The objective is arg min over R1, R2 ∈ Stiefel manifold (the set of orthonormal matrices) of L_Q(R1, R2 | W, X) — the *task loss (cross-entropy) of the quantised network* on calibration data, with pretrained weights W frozen. Optimising while staying exactly orthonormal is done with **Cayley SGD** (Li et al., 2020): the update is parameterised through a skew-symmetric matrix Y built from the projected gradient, and the Cayley transform ΔR(Y) = (I − αY/2)(I + αY/2)⁻¹ is guaranteed orthonormal, so R′ = ΔR(Y)R never leaves the manifold; the inverse is evaluated by fixed-point iteration using only matmuls, at ~2× the per-step cost of naive SGD. Because every iterate is exactly orthogonal, the full-precision network's output is unchanged at every step of training — learning touches *only* quantisation error, never the underlying model. R1 + R2 together are ~0.26% of the model's parameter count.

**What is learned vs fixed, and the quantiser stack.** Learned: R1, R2 (initialised as random Hadamard matrices; ablations show the result is robust to initialisation, including dense-orthogonal starts). Fixed: R3, R4 (Hadamard), all model weights, and the quantisation scheme itself. A subtle and important protocol choice: rotations are optimised against a network in which **only activations are quantised** (weights held at 16-bit during rotation learning), because GPTQ will be applied to the rotated weights afterwards and handles weight error itself — Table 3 of the paper shows this "Cayley on 16-4" protocol beats optimising against the fully quantised network by ~3 points (64.1 vs 61.0 average on LLaMA-2-7B W4A4). After learning, GPTQ is applied to the rotated weights in the standard way.

**Training cost, as reported.** 800 samples from WikiText-2, 100 Cayley-SGD iterations, learning rate 1.5 decaying linearly to 0. Wall-clock: ~13 / 18 / 30 minutes for LLaMA-3 1B / 3B / 8B; ~25 / 30 minutes for LLaMA-2 7B / 13B; ~16 minutes for Mistral-7B; **~3.5 hours for LLaMA-2-70B**. This is a calibration-stage cost, paid once per (model, bit-configuration) — orders of magnitude below QAT, and comparable to a GPTQ pass.

## 4. Reported results — as reported, with context

All figures are the paper's own (v4). Models: LLaMA-2 7B/13B/70B, LLaMA-3/3.2 1B/3B/8B (and LLaMA-3-70B in the QuaRot comparison), Mistral-7B. Two instruments, kept separate per wave-5 discipline: **WikiText-2 perplexity** and **average accuracy over eight zero-shot commonsense tasks** (BoolQ, PIQA, SIQA, HellaSwag, WinoGrande, ARC-Easy, ARC-Challenge, OBQA).

**The headline — W4A4KV4, SpinQuant_had + GPTQ (paper Table 1).** FP16 references in parentheses.

| Model | 0-shot avg (FP16) | Wiki PPL (FP16) |
|---|---|---|
| LLaMA-2-7B | **64.0** (66.9) — gap 2.9 pts | **5.9** (5.5) |
| LLaMA-2-13B | **66.9** (68.3) — gap 1.4 pts | **5.3** (5.0) |
| LLaMA-2-70B | **71.2** (72.9) — gap 1.7 pts | **3.8** (3.3) |
| LLaMA-3-8B | **65.5** (69.6) — gap 4.1 pts | **7.3** (6.1) |
| Mistral-7B | **68.6** (71.0) — gap 2.4 pts | **5.8** (5.4) |

Context for the 2.9-point headline: under the same W4A4KV4 setting the paper's baselines collapse — RTN and GPTQ-without-rotation score ~35–37 average (perplexities in the hundreds-to-millions), SmoothQuant 39.0, and LLM-QAT — a *quantisation-aware training* method — 44.9. SpinQuant thus surpasses LLM-QAT by 19.1 points and SmoothQuant by 25.0 points on LLaMA-2-7B *without training any model weights*. At W4A8KV8, SpinQuant_no_had (no online transforms at all) narrows the Mistral-7B gap from 12.1 to 1.6 points, and its W4A8 perplexities are comparable to the weight-only specialists (AWQ, OmniQuant, QuIP#) despite also quantising activations — the paper's evidence that learned rotation does not trade weight quality for activation quality.

**Learned vs random — the ablation that is the paper's thesis (paper Table 2).** Average zero-shot accuracy, learned minus random-Hadamard at identical placements: with R1,R2 only, +0.9 to +3.3 points across LLaMA-3.2-3B, LLaMA-3-8B, Mistral-7B at W4A4(16/4); with R1–R4, +1.6 to +2.2 points on the LLaMA models — and **+15.7 / +16.2 points on Mistral-7B** (52.7 → 68.4 at W4A4KV16; 52.4 → 68.6 at W4A4KV4). The honest reading: the learned-over-random gain is usually single-digit, occasionally enormous, and the enormous case is exactly a case where the random draw was pathological — learning is as much *variance elimination* (the Cayley-optimised results show small spread across seeds, where random draws spread by 6–13 points) as it is mean improvement. The paper also reports the optimised rotation beating the *best of 100 random draws*, so the gain is not merely "avoid the bad seeds".

**Against QuaRot directly (paper Table 5).** LLaMA-3-8B W4A4KV4 with GPTQ: QuaRot 63.3 avg / 8.0 PPL → SpinQuant_had 65.5 / 7.3. The dramatic row is **LLaMA-3-70B**: QuaRot+GPTQ collapses to 65.1 avg with **20.2 perplexity** (and QuaRot+RTN to 41.3 avg / 92.4 PPL — the paper attributes drops of 28.1–33.2 points for QuaRot-RTN at 70B), while SpinQuant_had holds 69.3 avg / **5.5 PPL** against an FP16 reference of 74.5 / 2.8. Summarised in the abstract as reducing the full-precision gap by up to 45.1% relative to QuaRot on LLaMA-3-8B (the figure varies by model and instrument across the paper's tables — 30.2%/34.1% relative reductions are quoted elsewhere in it for other settings; treat any single percentage as setting-specific). SpinQuant also reports using **two** online Hadamard matrices per block to QuaRot's four.

**Speed, as reported.** End-to-end on a MacBook M1 Pro **CPU**, LLaMA-3-8B W4A8: FP16 177.15 ms/token; SpinQuant_no_had 58.88 ms/token (~3×); SpinQuant_had 63.90 ms/token — i.e. the online Hadamards cost **~8% latency** over the absorbed-only configuration. This is a single CPU data point offered as an overhead bound, not a serving benchmark; the paper's GPU latency detail is in its appendix and is kernel-implementation-dependent.

**Calibration/eval separation — the caution, stated plainly.** This is where wave-5 discipline bites. SpinQuant's rotations are *fitted* on **800 WikiText-2 samples**, and one of its two headline instruments is **WikiText-2 test perplexity**. The paper does not state that the 800 calibration samples are drawn exclusively from the WikiText-2 *training* split (QuaRot, by contrast, specifies train-split calibration for its GPTQ stage; SpinQuant's own GPTQ stage likewise uses the standard 128 WikiText-2 samples). The zero-shot downstream averages — the source of the 2.9-point headline — are on tasks disjoint from WikiText-2 and are therefore clean held-out evidence, and they corroborate the perplexity story, which is reassuring. But the perplexity gaps quoted to a tenth of a point (5.9 vs 5.5) come from an evaluation whose corpus family the method was fitted on, by a method with a per-model fitted component — precisely the configuration wave 5 taught us to discount. Read SpinQuant's *downstream* numbers as its proven result and its *perplexity* numbers as upper-bound-flavoured until replicated on an untouched corpus.

## 5. Position in the lineage

The rotation lineage, in order, with each step's exact delta:

1. **QuIP / QuIP# (wave 5)** — random orthogonal (randomised Hadamard) incoherence processing for **weights only**, transforms undone on activations at inference, aimed at 2-bit weight-only PTQ with lattice codebooks. Rotation as a *weight preconditioner*.
2. **QuaRot (this wave)** — the same randomised Hadamard idea carried **through the whole network** via computational invariance: rotations fused into weights so activations and KV cache live in the rotated basis, plus online Hadamards where fusion is impossible, enabling W4A4KV4 end-to-end with plain scalar quantisation. Rotation as an *architecture-wide basis change* — but still **random and fixed**.
3. **SpinQuant (this dossier)** — identical placements (R1/R2 are QuaRot's fused rotations; R3/R4 are QuaRot's online ones, kept Hadamard), with the fused rotations **promoted to learned parameters**, optimised on the Stiefel manifold against the quantised network's loss. Rotation as a *fitted component of the quantiser*.

What SpinQuant adds over both predecessors, precisely: (i) the demonstration that rotation choice is a first-order, high-variance determinant of quantised quality (up to 13 points) — a fact invisible in both QuIP# and QuaRot, which report single random draws; (ii) a tractable optimiser for that choice (Cayley SGD, ~0.26% extra parameters, minutes-to-hours per model); (iii) robustness evidence at the lineage's known weak points — LLaMA-3 models, which are harder to quantise, and 70B scale, where QuaRot's random draw can fail catastrophically (§4, Table 5 row) and the learned rotation does not. What it costs over QuaRot: a calibration stage and its data-dependence (QuaRot's rotation needs no data at all), a per-model optimisation run, and the eval-hygiene burden of §4 — a fitted quantiser must prove it has not fitted the benchmark. Against **SmoothQuant** the contrast completes the wave-5 triangle: migration fits a *diagonal* transform to calibration data; SpinQuant fits a *dense orthogonal* transform to calibration data. Both are data-fitted, but the orthogonal family preserves norms and full-precision equivalence exactly, which is why SpinQuant can fit aggressively (100 full-loss iterations) without the fitted transform itself becoming a source of full-precision drift — the failure mode that caps SmoothQuant's migration strength α.

## 6. Applicability

### (a) The GGUF world — labelled judgment

SpinQuant splits cleanly along the same seam as QuaRot, and the split is even more favourable here:

- **SpinQuant_no_had is the most GGUF-compatible idea in the entire rotation lineage — in form.** Learned R1 + R2 are fully absorbed: the deliverable is an ordinary full-precision checkpoint with different weight values, quantisable by the existing offline GGUF pipeline (Q4_K etc.) and runnable by an unmodified runner, with zero runtime cost and zero kernel work. No other wave-5/16 method offers a fitted improvement that arrives as *just weights*. If learned rotation improves weight-only scalar quantisation at all, this is a pure converter-side upgrade.
- **But the evidence for that specific claim is thin — labelled judgment.** SpinQuant's gains are measured in W4A4/W4A8 regimes where *activation* error dominates; at W4A8 its perplexities merely match the weight-only specialists, and the paper never evaluates "learned-rotated weights, FP16 activations, scalar weight-only quantisation" as a headline configuration against a strong GGUF-class baseline. The mechanism by which R1/R2 would help a weight-only runner is indirect (rotated weights are more incoherent, hence flatter per-group ranges for K-quant grouping), and QuIP#'s dossier already established that incoherence processing's weight-only payoff concentrates at 2–3 bits, not at Q4_K's operating point. Expected gain at Q4_K batch-1 decode: **small, unproven, and not where the paper's numbers live.**
- **SpinQuant_had inherits QuaRot's verdict unchanged:** its online Hadamards (R3/R4) only pay when activations/KV are quantised at runtime, which a weight-only GGUF runner does not do; at batch-1 decode the KV term matters only at long context, where GGUF's existing KV-quant options are the proportionate tool. The ~8% online-transform latency the paper measures would be spent buying accuracy the runner never lost.
- **The calibration cost lands differently in our world.** SpinQuant's 25 minutes (7B) to 3.5 hours (70B) of rotation learning is trivial for a model publisher and prohibitive as a per-user step — but GGUF conversion is already a publisher-side, offline act, so this is no obstacle in principle. The real obstacle is provenance: a SpinQuant-rotated GGUF is a *fitted* artifact whose quality claims need the §4 hygiene (held-out grading) that the stock conversion pipeline does not currently perform.

### (b) Aesir's compression school — a question, framed with its grading discipline

*Question:* should the compression school — which writes its own Q8_0/Q4_K packers and grades packed-versus-original — treat **learned rotation** as a legitimate experiment, beyond the fixed-Hadamard pre-pass posed in the QuaRot dossier §6(b)? Framed as a question, with its discipline:

- **The fixed-rotation experiment must come first.** Learned rotation is only interpretable against the random-rotation control: if a randomised Hadamard pre-pass gives the school's packer nothing, a learned rotation has no mechanism left to exploit in a weight-only setting, and SpinQuant's own thesis (learning beats the *best of 100 random draws*) defines the exact comparison — learned vs best-random, not learned vs one unlucky draw.
- **Grading discipline it would need, beyond the QuaRot experiment's:** (i) **strict three-way data separation** — rotation-learning data, packer calibration data (if any), and grading data pairwise disjoint, with the grading set untouched by every fitted component; SpinQuant's WikiText-2 overlap (§4) is the cautionary example of what this discipline exists to prevent, and the school should be able to state its splits in one sentence each; (ii) **raw-oracle grading** as per corpus discipline — teacher-forced rank/logit-gap of the packed-rotated artifact against the original model, since a learned rotation is exactly the kind of fitted component that can look good on perplexity of its own fitting corpus while drifting in logit space; (iii) **variance reporting** — at least several random-rotation seeds graded alongside the learned one, because SpinQuant's central finding is that single-draw comparisons in this family are meaningless; (iv) **cost accounting** — Cayley-SGD wall-clock and sample count published beside the grade, per the school rule that a fitted method's price is part of its result.
- **What a positive result would and would not mean:** it would license learned rotation as a *converter-side* technique for the school's own artifacts at the tested bit-width. It would not license importing SpinQuant's W4A4KV4 numbers into a weight-only provisioning decision — those numbers measure a different inference regime, and the ledger discipline (IDEA-LEDGER #42) is to provision by lineage-matched evidence only.
